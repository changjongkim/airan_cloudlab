\begin{abstract}
AI-and-RAN schedulers share GPUs between deadline-sensitive radio processing and external AI. Existing
resource shares and queue schedulers miss one class of work: until an optional neural receiver
resolves, its transport block retains a conventional-recovery obligation that is mandatory under a
future failure branch but absent from the current queue. Scheduling only visible work is unsafe;
reserving every possible recovery strands capacity after neural success.

We present \sys, a certified multi-GPU scheduler for this conditional-work problem. It represents
each unresolved obligation as recovery debt and jointly decides optional-receiver admission,
conventional-recovery timing, and bounded external-AI leases. Every accepted decision preserves an
executable all-fail schedule, and the same certificate follows the decision through IPC/P2P ownership,
CUDA launch, physical completion, and radio commit. A provenance-qualified envelope predicts whether
a state is safe-useful, safe-no-slack, mandatory-infeasible, or unqualified before execution.

Across 13,716 mandatory-feasible capacity points spanning one to four GPUs, conditional scheduling
exposes capacity beyond static reservation at 3,600 (26.2\%), while debt-blind scheduling has positive
violation probability at 4,338 (31.6\%). A frozen trace screen identifies 362 configurations with at
least 5\% scheduling headroom; a safe online greedy is within 5\% of the fixed-placement oracle at
361, while flexible recovery placement has positive development-only upper-bound gain at 203. The
original trace mode is a negative result: certificate-preserving recovery-first and \sys both deliver
349,387 timely tokens.

On two independent four-A100 nodes, physical boundary diagnostics produce 40/40 guard-and-deadline
violations for debt-blind execution and 0/120 for certificate-preserving decisions. A separate
qualified warm path executes 4,800 TensorRT \NRx requests, 1,312 shared-cuPHY recoveries, 1,077 Qwen
units, and 4,800 radio commits without an observed deadline or invariant violation. The scheduler
matches an exact checker on 16,023 states and all 180 prespecified physical boundary rounds; at 64
debts its p99 decision time is 1.494~ms. These results qualify synthetic modes rather than production
HARQ; the 4.5~ms testMAC and external-channel exit gates remain explicit limitations.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

AI-and-RAN consolidates radio physical-layer processing and external inference on shared accelerators.
The consolidation can reclaim idle GPU capacity, but it also couples a deadline-sensitive radio path
to kernels whose arrivals and execution times follow a different service objective. Existing systems
schedule GPU fractions, queued requests, accelerator placement, or bounded background units
~\cite{yinyangran,cloudric,interplay,distributedairan}. Optional neural reception adds a scheduling
state that none of these visible-work abstractions captures: future mandatory recovery.

Consider one uplink transport block (TB). A timely \NRx result may improve its decoding outcome, while
failure, lateness, or lost evidence leaves conventional decoding mandatory. Before that outcome is
known, the conventional path is absent from the execution queue but remains part of the radio
contract. Several cells can hold this obligation at once, and several cuPHY instances can send recovery to
the same cuPHY lane. A scheduler that sees an idle GPU can therefore admit external AI into time that
must remain available for correlated recovery.

Static reservation keeps every possible recovery slot until every neural outcome resolves. It is safe,
but it strands the capacity released by timely neural success. A useful scheduler must decide three
coupled questions: which speculative receivers to admit, where and when all unresolved
conventional jobs could still execute, and which external-AI units can complete without invalidating
that plan. Queue occupancy, MPS percentage, and independent per-instance schedules omit at least one of
these decisions.

\sys is a certified event-driven scheduler built around \emph{recovery debt}. Every unresolved optional
path owns one debt, and each scheduling decision must leave a global executable all-fail calendar over
the remaining debts. A success deletes one debt; failure, lateness, or missing evidence retains it.
The scheduler may retime recovery and lease external AI only when an independent verifier accepts the
joint schedule. MPS supplies concurrency, while the verified schedule supplies the radio contract.

A mathematical certificate alone is insufficient. In our implementation, a valid calendar became
stale before kernel launch, simultaneous outcomes produced order-dependent intermediate states, a
fixed lease omitted launch control, and reply loss left ownership ambiguous. \sys carries the same
certificate through CUDA, IPC/P2P, and distributed-control state using bounded revalidation, atomic
outcome batches, physical latest-start enforcement, generation-safe ownership, and fence-confirmed
retirement. These mechanisms are established individually. Their combination refines an AI-RAN
recovery invariant from admission to physical completion.

The paper makes four contributions:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Conditional-recovery scheduling model.} We formalize unresolved same-TB recovery as
  debt and quantify safe AI capacity across cells, outcome correlation, deadlines, recovery cost,
  cuPHY instances, and one to four GPUs. Closed-form homogeneous capacity matches exhaustive checking.
  \item \textbf{Certified multi-GPU scheduler.} An event-driven candidate scheduler jointly retimes
  recovery and leases external AI; an independent verifier accepts only schedules that preserve the
  all-fail radio branch. Seven measured counterexamples refine that decision through transport,
  worker epoch, CUDA fence, and single-commit state.
  \item \textbf{Predictive feasibility envelope.} We classify each qualified state as
  qualified-safe-useful (\QSU), qualified-safe-no-slack (\QSN), mandatory-infeasible (\MI), or
  unqualified (\UQ) before physical execution and validate unopened physical boundaries.
  \item \textbf{Scheduling evaluation with bounded claims.} Model, trace, exact-oracle, and two-node
  physical experiments separate safe capacity, decision quality, enforcement, and deployment scope.
  We retain the zero-gain trace result and do not claim an executed placement optimizer.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Claim boundary.}
The guarantee is conditional on the qualified bounds and provenance of the evaluated mode. \sys does
not claim that MPS provides hard isolation, that finite-sample maxima are worst-case execution times
(WCETs), or that the synthetic timing contract is a production HARQ deadline. Five lifecycle subsets
are qualified or partially qualified and five remain \UQ; the claim does not require or imply a 10/10
lifecycle matrix.

\section{Background and Motivation}

\subsection{AI-and-RAN on a shared GPU}

A shared AI-RAN node executes three scheduling classes: speculative \NRx, mandatory conventional
recovery, and external AI. Figure~\ref{fig:airan-background} follows one uplink TB through one release
period of the evaluated contract. Until the common cutoff shows a timely \NRx success, the TB owes a
conventional recovery that no GPU queue contains. The scheduler must therefore track both the radio
branch and the physical resource on which that branch may execute.

\noindent\textbf{MPS Execution.}
MPS permits CUDA processes to execute concurrently and limits a client's active-thread
percentage~\cite{nvidiampS}. These controls choose how visible kernels share a GPU; they do not bound
host scheduling, queueing, context lifecycle, allocation, or arbitrary kernel blocking. We therefore
use MPS as the execution mechanism and make scheduling admission depend on a separate timing contract.

\noindent\textbf{MIG-Off Reclamation.}
MIG provides spatial separation, while fixed partitions restrict reclaiming capacity released by a
successful optional receiver. Our MIG-off setting keeps this capacity schedulable and uses qualified
MPS modes for concurrency. A missing bound makes the mode \UQ. Capacity is therefore reclaimed only within
the placement and lifecycle for which service bounds were established.

\subsection{Optional \NRx creates future mandatory work}

An optional receiver is speculative radio execution. It can improve the TB outcome, but its dispatch
does not remove the requirement to produce a conventional result after failure, lateness, or lost
evidence. This asymmetry creates a job whose release depends on the future receiver outcome.

\noindent\textbf{Same-TB Dependency.}
Before the \NRx outcome resolves, conventional decoding is conditional in the observed execution and
mandatory in an allowed future branch. It therefore need not appear in the current GPU queue even
though the scheduler must keep it executable. This gap between queued work and obligated work is the
central scheduling problem.

\noindent\textbf{Shared Recovery.}
Several cells can select \NRx together, and their failures can be correlated. When their conventional
paths share one cuPHY lane, feasibility belongs to the union of their unresolved jobs. A schedule that
is valid at each cuPHY instance can therefore be invalid after the instances' jobs are composed.

\noindent\textbf{External-AI Admission.}
Qwen is a representative external workload that creates physical GPU contention; it is not an
AI-RAN-domain workload. Its deadline and complete launch-to-fence service form a third job class.
Admitting it before an unresolved radio outcome is useful only when the remaining conventional jobs
still have a feasible placement.

\subsection{Threat model and qualification}

\sys provides a conditional guarantee for an explicitly qualified mode. Qualification binds service
bounds to the environment in which they were measured and defines the events the runtime must contain.

\noindent\textbf{Mode Provenance.}
A mode fixes GPU topology, MPS settings, process lifecycle, worker epoch, memory residency, co-run
class, software, and finite-sample whole-path bounds. Changing any element requires requalification.
The synthetic release period and result expiry used in evaluation are harness parameters, not NR slot
or production HARQ timing.

\noindent\textbf{Contained Events.}
The runtime handles \NRx failure and lateness, stale and duplicate messages, bounded reply loss before
or after a control-plane effect, missing AI replies after physical completion, and same-worker channel
reconnection. It fails closed when terminal physical evidence is absent.

\noindent\textbf{Excluded Events.}
Arbitrary GPU hangs, driver reset, unbounded host suspension, production-clock uncertainty, and
unqualified workloads remain outside the claim. Mandatory offered load without an all-fail schedule is
\MI rather than a reason to weaken the safety check.

\subsection{Missing scheduling state}
\label{sec:requirements}

Visible queues and static reservations fail in opposite directions. Figure~\ref{fig:scheduling-problem}
separates the three scheduling failures that SoftWall must avoid: admitting AI into apparently idle
time needed by future recovery, reserving conditional work after it becomes unnecessary, and composing
locally valid calendars on one shared lane. These failures define the state and scope of the scheduler.

\paragraph{Takeaway 1: current idleness is not schedulable slack.}
An unresolved \NRx leaves mandatory work outside the current queue. A scheduler needs an explicit
representation of that work before it can safely place external AI.

\paragraph{Takeaway 2: worst-case reservation is safe but incomplete.}
Timely \NRx success deletes recovery work and changes the feasible schedule. A useful scheduler must
recompute the calendar at outcome events rather than hold every initial reservation until expiry.

\paragraph{Takeaway 3: feasibility follows the contended lane.}
Local admission cannot protect a shared cuPHY worker. The certificate must cover the global obligation
set and update all affected recovery placements in one transaction.

These scheduling rules are still insufficient unless they survive physical execution. \sys uses
bounded admission, list scheduling, generation checks, CUDA fences, IPC/P2P rings, and at-most-once
ownership to refine one conditional-recovery invariant. Table~\ref{tab:counterexamples} shows why
each refinement is necessary; Figure~\ref{fig:overall} later maps the resulting transaction to the
runtime. All measured results appear in Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}.

\noindent\textbf{From Local to Global.}
Recovery feasibility belongs to the contended physical lane. The certificate must cover the union of
obligations before optional endpoint or external-AI admission.

\noindent\textbf{From Decision to Launch.}
A certificate must remain current through control delay, outcome observation, and CUDA launch. A
lease therefore includes launch control and carries an absolute latest-start enforced by the worker.

\noindent\textbf{From Logical Completion to Physical Retirement.}
RPC success is not CUDA completion, and reply loss is not proof that no effect occurred. Generations,
terminal markers, and physical fences bind ownership to retirement. Section~\ref{sec:design} formalizes this invariant and maps it to executable components.

\section{SoftWall Design}
\label{sec:design}

\sys turns conditional recovery into explicit scheduling state and preserves that state until GPU
effects become physically unambiguous. This section first presents the complete scheduler, then
expands the two transitions that make its decisions safe, and finally gives the event algorithm and
capacity model used by the implementation.

\subsection{Overall architecture}
\label{sec:design-overall}

Figure~\ref{fig:overall} shows the scheduling loop on its runtime objects. Persistent MPS workers own
GPU work, while one host coordinator owns the per-TB decision pages, the recovery-debt table, the
recovery-lane certificate, and the verifier. The four numbered arrows mark the cross-layer steps
of one epoch, and the shared lane runs retained recoveries in certificate order. The same control
layer serves a single GPU, where CUDA IPC rings connect the workers, and several GPUs, where retained
debts from different cuPHY instances share one recovery lane over NVLink P2P.

\paragraph{Scheduling objects.}
The scheduler admits only information visible at the current event. A radio job and an external-AI
job are
\begin{equation}
 J_i=(r_i,d_i,h_i,c_i,K_i,q_i), \qquad
 A_a=(u_a,v_a,\ell_a,b_a,g_a),
\end{equation}
where $r_i,d_i$ are release and expiry, $h_i$ is the owning cuPHY instance, $c_i$ is the conventional
host-to-commit bound, $K_i$ contains eligible \NRx endpoints, and $q_i$ is optional radio value;
$u_a,v_a,\ell_a,b_a,g_a$ are AI arrival, timely value, deadline, physical-completion bound, and
recovery guard. Decisions cannot use future CRC, arrivals, realized service, or hidden channel labels.

\paragraph{Qualified modes.}
A number becomes scheduling authority only with matching provenance
\begin{equation}
 \pi=(\text{GPU},\text{placement},\text{MPS},\text{lifecycle},\text{co-run},\text{software}).
\end{equation}
A warm local measurement therefore cannot certify a cold, restarted, or remote path. The scheduler marks
a state \UQ whenever a required bound lacks the active provenance, even if a sample happens to finish
before its deadline.

\paragraph{Separation of proposal and authority.}
The candidate scheduler may use a fast list heuristic and may reject a feasible placement. It cannot
create a false-safe execution because only the verifier can commit a candidate. With this separation,
the evaluation compares decision quality with an exact oracle while safety remains independent of the
heuristic.

\subsection{Scheduling conditional recovery}
\label{sec:design-recovery}

An unresolved \NRx is a scheduling obligation even when no conventional kernel is queued. SoftWall
records that obligation as recovery debt
\begin{equation}
 R_i=(a_i(t),d_i-g_i,c_i,h_i),
\end{equation}
where $a_i(t)$ is its earliest legal start. For unresolved set $U(t)$, an all-fail certificate assigns
every $R_i$ a non-overlapping interval $[s_i,s_i+c_i]$ on its physical recovery lane, after release
and before $d_i-g_i$. Contending transport and AI blackout intervals belong to the same certificate.

\noindent\textbf{All-Fail Dominance.}
\begin{proposition}[All-fail dominance]
If a timely \NRx success only deletes its conventional recovery and does not increase any remaining
job's demand, a feasible certificate for $U(t)$ is feasible for every failure subset
$F\subseteq U(t)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Delete successful requests from the all-fail schedule. Release, expiry, capacity, and non-overlap
constraints of every retained interval remain unchanged.
\end{proof}
The reduction replaces $2^{|U|}$ outcome schedules with one executable witness. It does not apply when
success creates new mandatory work or changes another service bound; that case needs a new mode.

\paragraph{Mandatory-first transition.}
Figure~\ref{fig:atomic-replan}a reserves $R_i$ before optional dispatch. Endpoint rejection therefore never
removes the conventional path. All outcomes observed at one cutoff enter one transition: timely
success deletes debt, while failure, lateness, and missing evidence retain it. Sequential replay is
invalid because it can manufacture intermediate states that did not coexist.

\paragraph{Global atomic state.}
Certificate scope follows the contended lane: disjoint lanes compose by union, while cuPHY instances
sharing a recovery lane require one global obligation set. The complete runtime state is
\begin{equation}
 X_t=(S_t,Q_t,T_t,L_t,E_t,C_t,G_t),
\end{equation}
where $S$ is the recovery schedule, $Q,T$ endpoint and transport credits, $L$ AI leases, $E$ fences
and worker epochs, $C$ radio commit state, and $G$ multi-instance AI ownership. Figure~\ref{fig:atomic-replan}b
commits a private $X'$ only when
\begin{align}
\mathrm{VALID}(X') \equiv {}& \mathrm{ExecutableRecovery}(S') \land
 \mathrm{UniqueCredits}(Q',T') \nonumber\\
 &\land\ \mathrm{TimelyAI}(L',E') \land \mathrm{CurrentGenerations}(X').
\end{align}
Failure leaves $X_t$ intact, eliminating partial calendar updates and orphan AI leases.

\noindent\textbf{Safety Invariant.}
\begin{invariant}[Certificate-to-execution refinement]
For every admitted radio request, every allowed unresolved-outcome branch retains a collision-free
conventional path completing by expiry. No endpoint, transport, AI, or output credit is reused before
matching terminal physical evidence, and each request commits at most one radio result.
\end{invariant}

\subsection{Refining schedules to physical completion}
\label{sec:design-refinement}

A feasible host calendar is insufficient unless launch and ownership obey it. Figure~\ref{fig:refinement}
follows one AI lease from commit through the latest-start gate, CUDA execution, and the marker that
retires it, together with the injected faults. The mechanism closes the interval between a scheduling
decision and the CUDA effect it authorizes.

\paragraph{Bounded launch.}
Prepare creates one unlaunched token carrying the calendar generation, worker epoch, ring and
transport credit, and absolute latest-start. After that time, a worker emits a qualified non-launch
marker and does not execute the stale schedule. The executor runs only the first non-preemptive action,
observes its terminal event, and returns control to the scheduler.

\paragraph{Physical retirement.}
RPC completion is not GPU completion. Leases and credits therefore remain owned until a matching CUDA fence.
A timeout without physical proof quarantines the generation and closes new optional admission. Stale,
duplicate, and reply-lost messages cannot retire newer ownership, and a transport-copy fence cannot
stand in for receiver or recovery completion.

\paragraph{Distributed ownership.}
One global AI queue may serve several cuPHY instances, but each instance may hold at most one unlaunched token in
an ownership epoch. Commit transfers launch authority only after local and global certificates remain
current. Same-worker reconnect carries the generation through bounded markers; full process
replacement without durable reconciliation remains \UQ.

\paragraph{Why IPC/P2P is part of scheduling.}
CUDA IPC and NVLink P2P avoid a host-staging copy, but their main design role is explicit ownership.
Each ring slot has one generation, one physical completion state, and one scheduler-visible retirement
event. Transport is therefore charged inside the qualified service path and is not treated as a
zero-cost link between an abstract scheduler and cuPHY/\NRx.

\subsection{Certified event-scheduling algorithm}
\label{sec:design-algorithm}

SoftWall is an event-driven scheduling algorithm with a proof-carrying commit point. Algorithm~1
applies simultaneous outcomes, restores an all-fail calendar, and then searches currently visible AI
candidates. It rejects optional work whenever provenance, recovery feasibility, or ownership is not
provable.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\fbox{\begin{minipage}{0.94\linewidth}
\small
\textbf{Algorithm 1: \textsc{CertifyEvent}$(X_t,\mathcal{E}_t,\mathcal{A}_t,\pi)$}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item If any required bound disagrees with $\pi$, return \UQ and disable new optional work.
  \item Apply the event batch $\mathcal{E}_t$ atomically: reserve debt for new radio jobs; delete debt
        only for timely successes; retain debt for fail, late, or missing evidence.
  \item Place every retained debt on its qualified physical lane. If no all-fail calendar exists,
        return \MI and reject new \NRx and AI admission.
  \item Visit only visible AI jobs in deterministic policy order. For each job, construct $X'$ by
        jointly retiming recovery and adding one complete AI lease.
  \item If the independent verifier accepts $X'$, atomically commit it, dispatch its first action,
        and return \QSU. Otherwise discard $X'$ without changing $X_t$.
  \item If no AI candidate passes, commit the recovery-only calendar and return \QSN. A matching
        terminal event retires ownership and invokes \textsc{CertifyEvent} again.
\end{enumerate}
\end{minipage}}

\paragraph{Decision window.}
For $m_h$ equal-length recoveries compacted at expiry $d_h$ with guard $g_h$, let
$b_{\mathrm{eff}}$ include synchronous control, AI service, and completion guard. AI-first execution
is safe under the sufficient, mode-specific condition
\begin{equation}
 T_{\mathrm{dec},h}+b_{\mathrm{eff}}
 \le d_h-g_h-m_hc_h. \label{eq:window}
\end{equation}
The right side is the first recovery start retained by the certificate. The inequality therefore charges
decision and launch delay rather than assuming immediate dispatch.

\noindent\textbf{Current Idleness.}
\begin{proposition}[Current idleness is insufficient]
On one non-preemptive lane with common guard $D$, unresolved bounds $c_1,\ldots,c_m$, and AI bound
$b_{\mathrm{eff}}$, suppose mandatory-only execution is feasible but
\begin{equation}
  t+b_{\mathrm{eff}}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_i>D. \label{eq:debtblind}
\end{equation}
Then admitting AI from current idleness while ignoring unresolved recovery cannot guarantee the radio
contract.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose the allowed realization in which AI and every recovery take their declared bounds. The final
recovery completes after $D$ by Equation~\ref{eq:debtblind}; present idleness does not erase future
mandatory demand.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Safe reclaimable capacity.}
The same invariant predicts the capacity that the algorithm may expose. Let $W$ be the guard window,
$L$ the AI deadline, $a$ one complete AI transaction, $c$ one recovery bound, $M_g$ potential static
debts, and $F_g$ realized debts on lane $g$. With $[x]_+=\max(0,x)$,
\begin{align}
 K_{\mathrm{static}}&=\sum_g\left\lfloor\frac{[\min(L,W-M_gc)]_+}{a}\right\rfloor,\\
 K_{\mathrm{RF}}&=\sum_g\left\lfloor\frac{[\min(L,W)-F_gc]_+}{a}\right\rfloor,\\
 K_{\sys}&=\sum_g\left\lfloor\frac{[\min(L,W-F_gc)]_+}{a}\right\rfloor. \label{eq:capacity}
\end{align}
Static holds all potential debt; recovery-first executes realized debt before AI; SoftWall may put AI
first while retaining $F_gc$ before the guard. Multi-class BurstGPT batches use exact subset, lane,
order, and recovery-insertion enumeration. Failure probabilities predict expected value, while the
all-fail safety certificate is distribution-free.

\section{Implementation}
\label{sec:implementation}

The prototype binds the model to persistent CUDA workers and explicit control objects. Its purpose is
to make certificate state observable and enforceable across process and GPU boundaries.

\subsection{Runtime topology}

The implementation runs with MIG disabled and MPS enabled. cuPHY instances, neural endpoints,
conventional recovery, and external AI execute in separate persistent workers.

\noindent\textbf{Radio and AI Workers.}
Local and remote TensorRT \NRx workers hold preallocated bindings and CUDA graphs. Persistent
Aerial/cuPHY workers execute conventional recovery, while a Qwen2.5-1.5B worker executes bounded
prefill classes. Multi-instance modes direct conventional work to one shared recovery GPU. In the
evaluated placement (Figure~\ref{fig:overall}b), GPU0 and GPU1 host the cuPHY instances that own the TBs with a
50\% MPS thread limit, GPU3 hosts the \NRx endpoint, and GPU2 hosts the shared cuPHY lane and the
Qwen worker, which runs as a low-priority MPS client with a 20\% thread limit.

\noindent\textbf{Transport.}
CUDA IPC rings connect processes on one GPU, and NVLink P2P rings connect GPUs. Each slot carries a
request generation and physical completion state. Copy completion does not imply receiver or recovery
completion; the owning worker publishes a separate terminal fence.

\noindent\textbf{Controller.}
The controller stores recovery calendars, endpoint and transport credits, worker epochs, global AI
ownership, and single-commit state. Unix-domain RPC carries control messages, while CUDA-resident data
stays on IPC/P2P paths.

\subsection{Certificate engine}

The certificate engine separates schedule construction from admission authority. This separation
contains candidate-scheduler bugs and makes each decision independently checkable.

\noindent\textbf{Candidate Scheduler.}
The scheduler applies deterministic list orders to radio jobs and visible AI candidates. It emits a
complete schedule, ownership delta, and mode fingerprint. Exact search is used only as a small-state
oracle and is not an optimization contribution.

\noindent\textbf{Independent Verifier.}
The verifier checks job identity, release, qualified service, expiry, lane capacity, overlap, AI
blackout, credit uniqueness, and generation. The runtime cannot execute a candidate rejected by the
verifier. This interface also supports retrospective comparison against exact feasibility.

\noindent\textbf{Event Loop.}
The executor performs the first non-preemptive action in an accepted certificate, observes its
terminal event, and replans. It never reorders physical work from an unverified host-side queue.

\subsection{Measurement and mode separation}

Instrumentation distinguishes logical decisions, host progress, GPU execution, and physical
retirement. This distinction prevents a host timestamp from being reported as a kernel boundary.

\noindent\textbf{Recorded Events.}
The runtime records release, admission, dispatch, worker observation, CUDA completion, commit return,
request and payload identity, ring generation, worker epoch, and lease retirement. Nsight traces check
GPU ordering for selected modes.

\noindent\textbf{Python and Native Paths.}
The qualified C158-C162 path uses pyAerial with persistent workers. A later native C++ effort verifies
input-layout and decoder parity only. It is not timing-qualified and its measurements are never mixed
with the Python mode's service bounds.

\noindent\textbf{Artifact Binding.}
Source hashes, protocols, seeds, node exclusions, analyzer versions, failures, and result hashes are
preserved in manifests. Every empirical block in this paper maps to a machine-readable artifact and
field set.

\section{Evaluation Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

The evaluation follows the scheduler's claim chain. It first measures whether conditional scheduling
adds safe capacity and how close its online decisions are to an oracle. It then tests prediction,
physical enforcement, fault closure, and the boundaries beyond which the scheduler has no authority.

\subsection{Questions and metrics}

We organize the experiments around seven questions. Table~\ref{tab:rqs} connects each question to the
claim it can support.

\noindent\textbf{Safety Metrics.}
We count radio expiry misses, declared-bound violations, guard crossings, invalid certificates,
duplicate commits, stale generation acceptance, transport-credit reuse, and residual leases. Zero
observed events is reported as a finite-sample result rather than a probability or WCET claim.

\noindent\textbf{Utility Metrics.}
External-AI utility is deadline-compliant token value and completed request count. Radio utility is
correct TB outcome under the declared channel mode. Candidate-policy comparisons preserve the same
radio decisions and observable inputs.

\subsection{Testbed, workloads, and contract}

Authoritative campaigns use two independent nodes, each with four A100-SXM4-40GB GPUs connected by
NVLink. Facility identity is withheld for anonymous review. MIG is off and MPS is on.

\noindent\textbf{Radio and External AI.}
Radio execution uses NVIDIA Aerial/cuPHY and TensorRT \NRx. Qwen2.5-1.5B prefill creates external GPU
contention. BurstGPT supplies arrivals and token values from four non-overlapping public-trace
windows~\cite{burstgpt}; Qwen is not labeled an AI-RAN-domain workload.

\noindent\textbf{Qualified Synthetic Contract.}
The evaluated warm mode uses harness period/expiry \texttt{P180/D155}, with finite-sample whole-path
bounds $B_{\mathrm{NRx}}=45$~ms, $B_{\mathrm{conv}}=25$~ms,
$B_{\mathrm{AI}}(\text{context64})=35$~ms, and a 2~ms guard. The period is not an NR slot, and the
expiry is not a production HARQ deadline. Other AI contexts use their separately qualified work class.

\noindent\textbf{Channel Modes.}
Synthetic channels drive system qualification. A separate Sionna CDL-D/E mode tests radio value under
its own channel, radio, model, and precision contract. Aerial TDL-A and field IQ remain outside that
supported mode.

\subsection{Protocols and baselines}

Each authoritative campaign freezes its decision rule before opening the outcome. Development and
holdout runs use independent seeds and nodes where the gate requires them.

\noindent\textbf{Experimental Discipline.}
Protocols fix source hash, workload, bounds, seeds, node assignment, exclusions, analyzer, and stop
rule. Failed, negative, and \UQ runs stay in the ledger. A model-predicted boundary is frozen before
physical execution.

\noindent\textbf{Safe Baselines.}
\emph{Static global-safe} retains worst-case recovery reservation. \emph{Certificate-preserving
recovery-first} uses the same max-radio decisions, initial all-fail admission, arrivals, bounds,
workers, and single-commit rule as \sys, but drains unresolved recovery before external AI. \sys adds
AI-first placement only with a valid witness. The offline oracle sees the realized trace and supplies
an unattainable upper bound.

\noindent\textbf{Diagnostic Baselines.}
Debt-blind current-idle admission, local-only certificates, and uncontrolled MPS diagnose broken
assumptions. They are not safe performance comparators and are not used to claim throughput gains.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
The anonymous artifact package contains source, protocols, manifests, analyzers, exact checker,
independent verifier, result summaries, and figure builders. Hardware campaigns require proprietary
Aerial/cuPHY and TensorRT components; model, checker, trace, and analysis paths do not. Node names and
absolute paths are removed from the review artifact.

\paragraph{Use of generative AI.}
Generative-AI tools assisted with language editing, organization, bibliography normalization, and
consistency-checking scripts. The authors designed the system and model, fixed protocols, executed
experiments, inspected failures, and verified every claim against hashed artifacts. Generative AI
produced no experimental measurement or ground-truth label.

\subsection{RQ1: Debt awareness expands safe capacity}
\label{sec:eval-capacity}

Conditional recovery creates a three-way scheduling tradeoff. Static reservation is safe but can
strand capacity, debt-blind admission uses that capacity without protecting radio recovery, and
\sys reclaims it only when a witness schedule exists.

% provenance: C167_CAPACITY
\noindent\textbf{Model Grid.}
The closed-form and exact multi-class capacity model covers 21,384 points over cells, cuPHY instances, GPUs,
outcome probability and correlation, expiry, recovery bound, AI class, deadline, and offered load.
Mandatory recovery is infeasible at 7,668 points. Among the 13,716 feasible points, \sys exceeds
static reservation at 3,600 (26.2\%), recovery-first trails its oracle by at least 5\% at 2,604
(19.0\%), and debt-blind admission has positive violation probability at 4,338 (31.6\%). Exhaustive
order checking agrees with the compact capacity calculation in all 5,376 checked cases. The
representative point in Figure~\ref{fig:capacity}a shows that conditional release increases safe
capacity while static reservation remains at zero.

% provenance: C162_NECESSITY
\noindent\textbf{What the certificate prevents.}
E4 leaves two debts at 45~ms and exposes a context-256 request. Debt-blind admission permits a
bound-respecting completion 12~ms beyond the 153~ms guard. E6b leaves one debt and makes context-64
unsafe at 89~ms by one millisecond. A prespecified diagnostic makes these allowed service vectors
physical as a bound-padded diagnostic. Across two independent nodes,
debt-blind E4 violates both guard and deadline in 40/40 selected rounds, and executing rejected E6b as
a shadow violates the guard in 40/40. \sys rejects both, admits the adjacent 88~ms point, and records
0/120 selected safety violations (95\% rule-of-three upper bound 2.5\%). Jointly bound-valid counts are
39/39, 39/39, and 0/119, respectively.

\noindent\textbf{Bound Sensitivity.}
The witnesses use the qualified $B_{\mathrm{conv}}=25$~ms contract. E6b disappears at or below 24~ms,
while E4 remains until 19~ms. The observed recovery maximum is 13.465~ms, but a sample maximum cannot replace
a service contract; lowering the bound defines another mode and requires requalification. The padded
campaign is therefore a necessity diagnostic for the declared contract, not a production-rate or WCET
claim.

\paragraph{Takeaway 1.}
Recovery debt is actionable scheduling state. It converts the unsafe capacity used by current-idle
admission and the stranded capacity held by static reservation into capacity that can be admitted with
an explicit all-fail witness.

\subsection{RQ2: Decision quality and scheduling levers}
\label{sec:eval-decision}

Safe capacity is useful only if an online policy can place visible jobs well. We compare the candidate
policy with exact fixed-placement schedules, an offline trace oracle, and a development-only
flexible-placement upper bound.

% provenance: C174_HEADROOM
\noindent\textbf{Trace Headroom Screen.}
A frozen screen maps four BurstGPT calibration windows onto 2,592 topology, failure, recovery, SLO,
and offered-unit points; 2,052 are mandatory-feasible. The prespecified advance rule requires an
oracle-normalized gap of at least 5\% and a positive gap in every window. It advances 362 points:
298 at a 100~ms SLO and 64 at 250~ms, with none at 50~ms or 1~s. The 50~ms jobs rarely fit even with
future knowledge, while recovery-first catches up at 1~s. Figure~\ref{fig:capacity}b therefore locates
scheduling headroom in an intermediate deadline band.

% provenance: C175_LEVERS
\noindent\textbf{Lever Decomposition.}
A certificate-preserving EDF/value AI-first greedy lies within 5\% of the fixed-placement exact oracle
at 361 of the 362 advanced points; the remaining point has a 7.02\% gap. Flexible recovery placement
has a positive upper-bound gain at 203 points: 0/102 one-GPU, 145/175 two-GPU, and 58/85 four-GPU
points, with a 10.64\% median positive gain. Selecting the frozen Sionna strata to minimize debt under
zero or 0.02 absolute radio-value loss reduces no recovery debt. The result localizes remaining
structural opportunity to cross-GPU recovery placement. Because this oracle excludes movement cost
and physical qualification, it is a design target rather than an executed performance claim.

% provenance: C159_BASELINE
\noindent\textbf{Original-Mode Negative Result.}
The calibration-only exact screen gives static global-safe, recovery-first, \sys, and the oracle the
same radio decisions, arrivals, bounds, and one-Qwen-per-epoch capacity. After correcting a preserved
absolute-deadline interpretation defect without excluding a request or opening the holdout, exact
matching assigns 856 timely requests and 349,387 tokens to recovery-first, \sys, and the oracle.
Static reservation exposes no qualified same-epoch Qwen slot and returns zero. Charging every realized
recovery its full bound leaves recovery-first at 345,405 tokens, only 1.14\% below \sys and below the
prespecified 5\% gate.

\subsection{RQ3: Prediction accuracy and control cost}
\label{sec:eval-prediction}

The scheduler must know before launch whether a candidate belongs to the safe-useful region. We test

admitted in 30/30, while 89~ms or later is rejected in 30/30. The campaign executes 720 actual \NRx
requests, 330 recoveries, 90 Qwen units, and 720 commits without an observed deadline miss.

\noindent\textbf{Variable-Class Workload.}
The frozen \texttt{P180/D155} campaign runs 600 epochs on each of two nodes with 16--512-token Qwen

\subsection{RQ5: Faults preserve schedule ownership}
\label{sec:eval-fault}

Logical feasibility is insufficient when a stale message or lost reply can retire the wrong physical
credit. We therefore combine finite-state checking with physical fault injection.

% provenance: C160_FAULT
\noindent\textbf{State and Physical Campaigns.}
A finite-state audit checks 51 combinations of epoch, generation, fence, and marker state without an
invariant violation. The A0--A6 physical campaign covers clean operation, correlated \NRx failure,
reply delay before and after completion, stale and duplicate responses, terminal channel fault, and
post-fault continuation. Across qualified nodes it executes 700 epochs, 2,800 \NRx requests, 942
recoveries, and 2,800 commits with no observed deadline miss, including 40 replay pairs, four terminal
faults, and 260 continuation epochs.

\noindent\textbf{Fail-Closed Scope.}
One additional node violates the \NRx whole-path bound on its first round. We retain that run as \UQ
and do not pool it into the qualified aggregate. Fault containment therefore applies only to the
two qualified nodes and declared fault/lifecycle classes.

\paragraph{Takeaway 5.}
Generation-safe ownership and physical fences preserve the schedule through the tested ambiguity
classes. The 0/2,800 physical miss count has a 95\% rule-of-three upper bound of 0.107\%.

\subsection{RQ6: MPS and lifecycle define the mode}
\label{sec:eval-mps}

MPS controls concurrency without certifying time, and worker lifecycle changes the service
distribution on which a schedule relies. These mechanisms therefore belong to mode provenance rather
than replacing the scheduler's certificate.

% provenance: Q1_MPS_DIAGNOSTICS
\noindent\textbf{MPS Diagnostics.}
A separate-client overrun produces 2/1,500 deadline misses at a 100\% cap and 1/1,500 at a 20\% cap.
A matched lifecycle comparison observes 0/10,000 misses for a CPU socket sham and 12/10,000 for the
GPU/MPS-client arm. Figure~\ref{fig:qualification}a reports the two campaigns separately; it does not
imply a monotonic cap effect.

% provenance: C164_LIFECYCLE
\noindent\textbf{Lifecycle Envelope.}
The matrix contains five qualified or partial subsets and five \UQ modes; the claim does not require or
imply a 10/10 lifecycle matrix. Qualified evidence covers warm
persistent execution, the first request after 30~s idle, MPS restart followed by requalification,
radio continuity during Qwen reload, and same-worker reconnect. Optional-service availability during
restart or reload is outside the claim. Process/model cold, 5~min and 30~min idle, GC-on, and worker
process replacement remain \UQ. A single-node replacement result is ``Development canary only''
because it lacks an independent holdout. Durable journal recovery would be a new mechanism and remains
outside this paper.

\paragraph{Takeaway 6.}
Cap selection and current queue state do not authorize admission. Scheduling bounds remain valid only
for the topology, placement, software, and lifecycle fingerprint that produced them.

\subsection{RQ7: Deployment exit gates}
\label{sec:eval-exit}

The synthetic contract does not establish production timing. We use a vendor integration threshold to
falsify candidate implementations while keeping target-DU timing as the first unresolved gate.

% provenance: P2_FAST_PATH_AND_STAGE
\noindent\textbf{Production Timing.}
Aerial testMAC expects CRC-bearing UL indication by $T0+4.5$~ms. Under clean, warm, no-AI conditions,
local wait-then-recover, same-GPU speculative, and precomputed cross-GPU paths complete 0/1,000 within
4.5~ms. Raw-IQ P2P moves channel estimation through CRC to GPU1 and reaches 852/1,000; a shared
cuPHY/TensorRT stream reaches 885/1,000. The frozen persistent, stream-ordered fast path reaches
995/1,000 with 3.813~ms median and 3.995~ms p99, but five late completions reject the gate. The holdout
stays closed. TestMAC is a controlled developer L2 rather than a field-DU $d_{\mathrm{MAC}}$ source.

\noindent\textbf{Mechanism Attribution.}
A 300-request profile attributes the remaining tail to remote channel estimation: GPU p50/p99/max are
0.866/4.873/9.592~ms and correlation with pair wall time is 0.9603. P2P copies average
36.261/17.150~$\mu$s, while derate matching exposes a 1,060.105~$\mu$s host call despite 0.168~ms GPU
p99. TensorRT enqueue averages 7.443~$\mu$s and its graph has 0.886~ms GPU p99. These profiles are
mechanism evidence rather than qualification or WCET.

\noindent\textbf{Unsupported External TDL-A.}
External Aerial TDL-A remains unsupported. Across raw and normalized inputs, public-reference radio
settings, one-antenna frequency/time channel execution, and a public-notebook-style FP32 engine,
conventional decoding succeeds in every ten-TB
arm and \NRx succeeds in none. Aerial TDL-A remains \UQ.

% provenance: P3_CHANNEL_HOLDOUT
\noindent\textbf{Supported Sionna Holdout.}
A fixed development matrix selects Sionna CDL-D/E at 100~ns and retains A/B/C failures. A disjoint-seed
holdout uses five Es/No strata and 50 blocks per stratum per model. Both pipelines pass 50/50 at
10~dB. CDL-D yields 153/250 conventional and 164/250 neural successes, with 16 neural-only and five
conventional-only outcomes. CDL-E yields 155/250 and 163/250, with 15 neural-only and seven
conventional-only outcomes.

\paragraph{Takeaway 7.}
The aggregate 31 neural-only versus 12 conventional-only outcomes give a two-sided exact
$p=0.00540$: optional \NRx has conditional radio value in the frozen Sionna CDL-D/E mode. That channel has
not passed production timing or integrated service-vector qualification, while the best 4.5~ms
testMAC candidate still misses 5/1,000. These failed gates bound the scheduler claim to qualified
synthetic modes.

~\cite{concordia,daris,reef,xsched,nvtaskset}. Their scheduling units are resources or queued jobs,
while recovery debt is future conventional work tied to one unresolved radio result.

ARCHES selects PHY experts, OCUDU exposes in-DU timing classes, and SMEC coordinates RAN/edge SLOs
~\cite{arches,ocudu,smec}. They rule out novelty claims for coexistence, fallback, or deadline
scheduling. Their public designs do not expose a cross-instance all-fail recovery calendar atomically
joined to an external-AI lease through physical completion.

\noindent\textbf{Scoped Difference.}
Across public designs reviewed through September 2026, we found no system combining an executable
all-fail calendar for unresolved same-TB recovery, external-AI leasing through physical retirement,
and a provenance-qualified predictive envelope. This is a scoped literature finding, not proof about
unpublished work or other terminology.

\section{Limitations}

\noindent\textbf{Timing Contract.}
\texttt{P180/D155} is a synthetic harness contract: $P$ is the inter-release period, not an NR slot
duration, and \texttt{D155} is not a production HARQ deadline. A production \texttt{d\_MAC} trace
builder and validator pass 24 unit tests, but a target DU/FAPI trace is unavailable. A new vector
requires physical requalification; we make no production HARQ guarantee.

\noindent\textbf{Finite-Sample Qualification.}
Zero observed violations establish neither WCET nor zero failure probability. Bounds belong to one
hardware, software, placement, workload, and lifecycle fingerprint. All authoritative hardware
results use A100s; NVLink P2P does not establish cross-family or inter-node data-plane validity.

% provenance: C158_LONG_TAIL
\noindent\textbf{Unresolved Long Tail.}
In C158 attempt 4, one \NRx completion reached 350.948\,ms while the 400-request run's p99 was
8.111\,ms; the run had 4/400 deadline misses. Targeted idle, MPS restart and requalification, Qwen
reload, and reconnect campaigns did not reproduce it. Its cause remains unresolved; GPU/driver hangs
remain outside the fault model.

\noindent\textbf{Workload Mapping.}
BurstGPT supplies real arrivals and token lengths; Qwen2.5-1.5B prefill is a representative external
workload. Token buckets and AI deadlines are experimental mappings; other workloads need new bounds
and replay qualification.

\section{Conclusion}

\sys represents optional-\NRx recovery debt, maintains a multi-cell all-fail certificate, and leases
external AI through physical completion. Measured counterexamples derive its refinement chain, while
the capacity model locates safe reclamation and identifies cross-GPU recovery placement as the main
remaining headroom. Two-node evidence validates qualified synthetic modes; finite samples,
unsupported channels, and absent target-DU timing establish neither WCET nor production HARQ.
