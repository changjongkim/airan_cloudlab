\begin{abstract}
AI-and-RAN platforms seek to run radio processing and external AI services on shared accelerators.
GPU concurrency mechanisms such as NVIDIA Multi-Process Service (MPS), however, do not by themselves
provide a temporal contract for the radio path. The problem becomes sharper when a neural receiver is
optional: until its result is known, the same transport block retains a conventional-receiver recovery
obligation. Across cells and RAN homes, these contingent obligations can collide on shared recovery
resources even though they have not entered a runtime queue.

We present \sys, a runtime substrate that represents unresolved conventional work as recovery debt.
\sys continuously maintains an executable all-fail schedule for every unresolved neural receiver. It
may retime recovery intervals and admit a bounded external-AI unit only through one generation-safe
transaction that preserves this certificate. The transaction covers endpoint and transport credits,
external-AI ownership, physical latest-start checks, CUDA completion fences, and single radio commit.
We also derive a provenance-qualified feasibility envelope that distinguishes safe-and-useful,
safe-without-slack, mandatory-infeasible, and unqualified modes.

Two prespecified boundary states show why current GPU idleness is insufficient: debt-blind AI
admission can cross the radio guard by 12\,ms and 1\,ms under the qualified contract, while \sys
rejected all 60 corresponding requests before physical launch. On two independent $4\times$A100 nodes with MIG
disabled and MPS enabled, a warm path executed 4,800 actual TensorRT neural-receiver requests, 1,312
shared-cuPHY recoveries, 1,077 Qwen units, and 4,800 radio commits without an observed deadline, bound,
transport, credit, or commit violation. A seven-class fault campaign executed another 2,800
neural-receiver requests and 942 recoveries with no observed deadline miss. The model agreed with an
exact checker on 16,023 qualified states and with 180 prespecified physical boundary rounds; a
certified 64-debt scheduler had 1.494\,ms p99 decision latency. A strong certificate-preserving
recovery-first baseline and an offline oracle found no material throughput advantage, so we make no
optimizer claim. \sys instead identifies when conditional sharing is certifiably safe, when it has no
useful slack, and when the mode must be rejected or requalified. These timing claims are finite-sample
qualifications of synthetic \texttt{P180/D155} modes. Applying the same discipline to Aerial
testMAC's 4.5\,ms UL-indication threshold rejected every tested implementation; the best raw-IQ
same-stream two-GPU prototype completed 885/1,000 clean requests in time, with the remaining tail
localized to remote cuPHY channel estimation. External Aerial TDL-A remains unqualified, but a
prespecified disjoint-seed Sionna CDL-D/E holdout passed: among 500 paired trials, low-SNR outcomes
contained 31 \NRx-only versus 12 conventional-only correct blocks. These gates bound the present
claim and identify the required production path and supported channel domain.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Modern base stations increasingly use GPUs for both the physical layer and AI services. This creates
an appealing utilization opportunity: serve inference requests while radio demand leaves compute idle.
Existing AI-and-RAN systems allocate GPU fractions, place work across accelerator pools, reclaim slack,
or split background work into bounded units~\cite{yinyangran,cloudric,interplay,distributedairan}.
Those mechanisms are valuable, but they do not resolve a semantic dependency introduced by an optional
neural receiver.

Consider an uplink transport block (TB) for which a conventional receiver is mandatory and a \NRx path
is optional. A timely successful \NRx result can improve radio utility. A failed, late, or lost result
must still leave enough time to execute conventional decoding and publish one radio result. Before the
\NRx outcome is known, conventional work is therefore a \emph{future mandatory job}. It may not appear
in any GPU queue, yet scheduling an external-AI kernel into its eventual recovery interval can make the
radio deadline infeasible.

This is a multi-request problem. Several cells may select \NRx at once and fail together. Several RAN
homes may send their conventional recoveries to one shared cuPHY worker. Local schedules can each look
feasible while their union overloads the shared lane. For example, with 25\,ms recoveries and a
100\,ms deadline, homes with three and two obligations are locally feasible but require 125\,ms
together. GPU allocation percentages and queue-aware placement cannot detect this state unless the
runtime represents obligations that have not yet materialized as queued work.

\sys treats each unresolved optional path as \emph{recovery debt}. At every state transition, it
retains an executable schedule for the branch in which all unresolved \NRx requests fail. This single
all-fail certificate covers every failure subset when successful outcomes only delete mandatory work.
An external-AI unit is admitted only if its complete transaction---control, physical execution, and
guard---can be inserted while leaving the recovery certificate valid.

The certificate must survive implementation details. We found counterexamples in which a safe plan
became stale before dispatch, simultaneous outcomes were replayed through a state that never existed,
launch control was omitted from an AI lease, and a second prepare created an untracked broker-held
token. \sys closes these gaps with bounded revalidation, atomic common-cutoff outcome batches,
physical latest-start enforcement, single-token ownership, and fence-confirmed retirement. These
mechanisms are conventional in isolation. Their role here is to refine one radio recovery certificate
through CUDA, IPC/P2P, and distributed control state.

We make four contributions:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Certificate-to-execution refinement.} We derive seven requirements from measured
  counterexamples and implement atomic recovery retiming and external-AI leasing across RAN homes,
  shared cuPHY recovery, local/remote \NRx endpoints, and physical completion lifecycles.
  \item \textbf{Predictive feasibility envelope.} We classify qualified-safe-useful (\QSU),
  qualified-safe-no-slack (\QSN), mandatory-infeasible (\MI), and unqualified (\UQ) states using debt,
  decision time, AI class, capacity, and provenance. An independent verifier prevents heuristic
  schedules from causing unsafe admission.
  \item \textbf{Conditional-recovery model.} We specialize established primary/backup reasoning to
  same-TB optional reception, shared recovery, and a third external-AI class. All admissible \NRx
  failure subsets reduce to one executable all-fail certificate under explicit assumptions.
  \item \textbf{Physical evaluation and negative policy result.} We evaluate actual TensorRT \NRx,
  NVIDIA Aerial/cuPHY recovery, Qwen inference, MPS, CUDA IPC, and NVLink P2P on independent A100
  nodes. Strong baselines and an offline oracle show no material throughput headroom in the evaluated
  trace; we therefore separate the substrate contribution from optimizer claims.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Claim boundary.}
\sys does not claim that MPS provides hard isolation, that measured maxima are worst-case execution
times (WCETs), or that the synthetic deadline is a production HARQ contract. Its guarantees are
conditional on an explicitly qualified mode and service bounds. Five lifecycle subsets are qualified
or partially qualified for the paper; five remain \UQ. The claim does not require or imply a 10/10
lifecycle matrix.

\paragraph{Artifact plan.}
Upon acceptance, we will release the controller and verifier, finite-state and exact model checkers,
machine-readable protocols and analyzers, sanitized timing records, and manifests that bind source to
reported results through a long-lived archival repository. Proprietary Aerial/cuPHY, TensorRT, or model
components will be accompanied by acquisition and build instructions rather than redistributed.

\section{Background and Problem}

\subsection{AI-and-RAN on shared GPUs}

MPS allows CUDA processes to execute concurrently and can limit a client's active-thread
percentage~\cite{nvidiampS}. It does not bound host scheduling, queueing, context lifecycle, memory
allocation, or arbitrary kernel blocking. In our early experiments, separate-client overrun produced
deadline misses under both 100\% and 20\% active-thread caps. A CPU socket sham produced 0 misses in
10,000 requests, while a GPU/MPS client-lifecycle arm produced 12. We use MPS as a concurrency
mechanism and construct the timing contract above it.

MIG can provide stronger spatial separation, but it fixes resource partitions and is not the object
of this work. We intentionally study MIG-off modes in which conditional capacity may be reclaimed. A
mode that cannot obtain qualified bounds remains \UQ instead of inheriting a bound from another
lifecycle or placement.

\subsection{Optional NeuralRx is speculative radio execution}

For one radio request, \NRx is not interchangeable with a background AI job. It may improve the radio
result, but a failed or late \NRx leaves conventional decoding mandatory. A correct runtime must decide
three linked questions:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Which optional \NRx endpoints can return before the fallback cutoff?
  \item Can every unresolved request still recover if all accepted \NRx paths fail together?
  \item Which bounded external-AI units can execute without invalidating that recovery plan?
\end{enumerate}
Current queue contents answer none completely because the recovery jobs are contingent. Static
worst-case reservation is safe but can retain recovery capacity after successful \NRx outcomes. \sys
reclaims that capacity only after observing the corresponding physical outcome.

\subsection{Threat model and qualification}

Each mode specifies the GPU topology, MPS settings, process lifecycle, worker epochs, memory residency,
co-run class, and finite-sample whole-path bounds. \sys handles \NRx failure and lateness, stale and
duplicate messages, reply loss at bounded control points, and loss of an AI reply after physical
completion. It fails closed when completion evidence is missing.

We exclude arbitrary GPU hangs, driver reset, unbounded host suspension, production-clock uncertainty,
and workloads outside qualified classes. Mandatory offered load that has no all-fail schedule is
reported as \MI rather than rescued by dropping a safety check.

\section{Failure-Derived Design Requirements}

\sys uses known primitives: MPS, bounded admission, list scheduling, generation checks, CUDA fences,
IPC/P2P rings, and at-most-once ownership. Novelty does not follow from listing them. It follows from
the cross-layer invariant they refine and the measured counterexamples that break naive composition.

\section{Model}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Jobs, modes, and provenance}

A radio request is
\begin{equation}
  J_i=(r_i,d_i,h_i,c_i,K_i,q_i),
\end{equation}
where $r_i$ is release, $d_i$ is radio-result expiry, $h_i$ is its recovery home, $c_i$ is a qualified
conventional host-to-commit bound, $K_i$ is the set of eligible \NRx endpoints, and $q_i$ is optional
radio value. Endpoint eligibility includes queue, ring, transport, \NRx, observation, and commit costs
under the current co-run mode.

An external-AI request is
\begin{equation}
  A_a=(u_a,v_a,\ell_a,b_a,g_a),
\end{equation}
where $u_a$ is visible arrival, $v_a$ is timely value, $\ell_a$ is deadline, $b_a$ is a qualified
physical-completion bound for its work class, and $g_a$ is the guard before recovery.

A bound is usable only when its provenance tuple matches the active mode:
\begin{equation}
 \pi=(\text{GPU},\text{placement},\text{MPS},\text{lifecycle},\text{co-run},\text{software}).
\end{equation}
This rule prevents a warm, garbage-collection-off measurement from silently qualifying a cold,
GC-on, remote, or restarted mode.

\subsection{Recovery debt and all-fail certificates}

While outcome $i$ is unresolved, it induces recovery job
\begin{equation}
  R_i=(a_i(t),d_i-g_i,c_i,h_i).
\end{equation}
For unresolved set $U(t)$, an all-fail certificate assigns every $R_i$ an interval
$[s_i,s_i+c_i]$ satisfying release, deadline, resource-capacity, and non-overlap constraints. Shared
AI blackouts and transport credits are included wherever they contend with recovery.

\begin{proposition}[All-fail dominance]
If a successful \NRx only deletes its conventional recovery and does not increase any remaining job's
demand, a feasible certificate for $U(t)$ is feasible for every failure subset $F\subseteq U(t)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Delete from the all-fail schedule the intervals corresponding to successful requests. Releases,
deadlines, capacities, and non-overlap of the retained intervals are unchanged.
\end{proof}

The reduction avoids enumerating $2^{|U|}$ failure outcomes. It does not apply when success creates new
mandatory work or changes another recovery's bound; such a mode needs a different proof.

\subsection{Shared recovery and atomic state}

Certificates over disjoint recovery resources compose by union. They do not compose when homes share
a recovery GPU. \sys therefore constructs a global certificate over the union of obligations and
commits all obligation, calendar, and AI-lease changes in one generation. The three-home plus two-home
example in Section~1 is a minimal local-safe/global-unsafe counterexample.

The runtime state is
\begin{equation}
  X_t=(S_t,Q_t,T_t,L_t,E_t,C_t,G_t),
\end{equation}
where $S$ is the recovery schedule, $Q$ endpoint credits, $T$ IPC/P2P transport generations, $L$ AI
leases, $E$ physical fences and worker epochs, $C$ radio commit state, and $G$ multi-home AI ownership.
\sys builds a private candidate $X'$ and commits it only when
\begin{align}
\mathrm{VALID}(X') \equiv {}& \mathrm{ExecutableRecovery}(S') \land
 \mathrm{UniqueCredits}(Q',T') \nonumber\\
 &\land\ \mathrm{TimelyAI}(L',E') \land \mathrm{CurrentGenerations}(X').
\end{align}
Otherwise $X_t$ is unchanged. A lease retires only through the matching physical completion fence or
a qualified non-launch record. A timeout without evidence quarantines its credit.

\begin{invariant}[Certificate-to-execution refinement]
For every admitted radio request, all allowed unresolved-outcome branches retain a collision-free
conventional path completing by the request expiry. No endpoint, transport, AI, or output credit is
reused before matching physical terminal evidence, and each request commits at most one radio result.
\end{invariant}

\subsection{Decision-window condition}

Suppose $m_h$ equal-length recoveries remain compacted at deadline $d_h$, with guard $g_h$. At decision
time $T_{\mathrm{dec},h}$, let $b_{\mathrm{eff}}$ include synchronous control, the AI work-class bound,
and completion guard. A sufficient condition for executing AI first is
\begin{equation}
 T_{\mathrm{dec},h}+b_{\mathrm{eff}}
 \le d_h-g_h-m_hc_h. \label{eq:window}
\end{equation}
The right side is the first recovery start in the retained certificate. Equation~\ref{eq:window}
explains why identical debt and AI class can be safe at 88\,ms and unsafe at 89\,ms. It is a
mode-specific sufficient condition, not a general online scheduling optimum.

\begin{proposition}[Current idleness is insufficient]
Consider one non-preemptive recovery lane, a common radio guard boundary $D$, unresolved recoveries
with qualified bounds $c_1,\ldots,c_m$, and an external-AI transaction with bound
$b_{\mathrm{eff}}$. If mandatory-only execution is feasible but
\begin{equation}
  t+b_{\mathrm{eff}}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_i>D,
  \label{eq:debtblind}
\end{equation}
then a policy that admits AI at time $t$ from current GPU idleness while ignoring unresolved recovery
cannot guarantee the radio contract.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Choose the allowed realization in which the AI transaction and every recovery take their declared
bounds. Because the work is non-preemptive on the contended lane, the last recovery completes after
$D$ by Equation~\ref{eq:debtblind}. The lane may be physically idle at $t$; the contradiction comes
from future mandatory work rather than queued work.
\end{proof}

This proposition does not say that every sampled debt-blind run must miss. It says that the policy
cannot provide the qualified guarantee. A conservative controller can remain safe by validating the
initial all-fail admission and physically draining unresolved recovery before AI. \sys is needed when
the controller wants to place AI first without abandoning that guarantee.

\section{Design}

\subsection{Mandatory-first admission}

On radio release, \sys reserves conventional recovery before considering an optional endpoint. \NRx
is admitted only when its whole-path bound fits the fallback cutoff and the endpoint has a
generation-matching ring credit. The initial all-fail certificate therefore exists before speculative
radio work begins.

\subsection{Outcome-driven replanning}

All \NRx outcomes observed at a common cutoff are applied as one batch. Timely successes delete their
debts; failure, lateness, or missing evidence retains them. \sys rebuilds the unresolved calendar at
the current time and tests visible AI requests against the updated certificate.

Applying simultaneous successes one by one is unsafe as a decision procedure: an intermediate debt
set that never existed can be infeasible at the later host time even when the final observed set is
feasible. The batch transition avoids this order dependence.

\subsection{AI leasing and launch-time enforcement}

AI work is non-preemptive and admitted by work class. A certificate older than the bounded launch
control interval is discarded and recomputed. The worker receives an absolute latest-start timestamp
and does not launch a CUDA kernel after it. Empty non-launch leases still produce terminal evidence so
ownership can retire without guessing.

The transaction charges physical work and the control required to start it. This closes a
counterexample in which a fixed $[\mathrm{start},\mathrm{end}]$ lease was valid when computed but launch
control delayed the kernel into recovery time.

\subsection{Distributed ownership and fault containment}

One global AI queue can be offered to several RAN homes. A prepare creates at most one unlaunched token
per home. Commit grants launch authority only after a current local/global certificate is validated.
Reply loss after a broker-side effect makes the request ambiguous; \sys does not retry it as a new
request. The affected home closes new AI admission while its radio path and other homes continue.

Prepare, abort, commit, and complete carry generations and durable terminal markers in the qualified
same-worker reconnect subset. Process replacement and arbitrary crash-window recovery remain outside
the paper's claim.

\subsection{Certificate-conformant execution}

The executor performs only the next non-preemptive action from the returned certificate, observes the
event, and replans. It does not reorder recovery around AI using an unverified host-side plan. CUDA
events and worker-side synchronization establish physical completion before IPC buffers, ring credits,
leases, or radio output state are reused.

\section{Implementation}

\sys runs on NVIDIA A100 nodes with MIG disabled and legacy MPS enabled. The evaluated topology uses
local and remote TensorRT \NRx endpoints, CUDA IPC and NVLink P2P transports, persistent NVIDIA
Aerial/cuPHY conventional workers, and Qwen2.5-1.5B prefill as external AI. Multi-home modes send radio
requests to a shared conventional-recovery GPU. IPC provides process isolation and explicit buffer
ownership; P2P moves CUDA-resident data across GPUs without converting recovery semantics into an
implicit copy-completion assumption.

The controller maintains recovery calendars, endpoint/ring credits, worker epochs, transport
generations, global AI ownership, and single-commit state. A candidate scheduler constructs a schedule;
an independent verifier checks every job key, release, service duration, deadline, lane overlap, and AI
blackout before the runtime can use it. Exact search is a small-state oracle rather than a claimed
optimization contribution.

Instrumentation records host release and commit timestamps, request and payload identities, CUDA
completion, IPC/P2P generations, MPS lifecycle, and ownership transitions. Nsight traces validate GPU
ordering for selected modes. Source, protocols, seeds, node exclusions, analyzers, failures, and result
hashes are preserved in immutable manifests.

\section{Evaluation Methodology}

We ask seven questions: (1) why MPS alone is insufficient; (2) whether certificate-preserving
transactions execute on the actual \NRx--cuPHY--Qwen path; (3) whether specified control/data faults
are contained; (4) whether the model predicts unopened physical boundaries; (5) whether certified
construction scales beyond exact search; (6) whether retiming yields material AI value over a strong
safe baseline; and (7) which lifecycle modes are qualified.

\paragraph{Testbed and workload.}
Authoritative campaigns use two independent nodes, each with four A100 GPUs and NVLink connectivity.
MIG is off and MPS is on. Radio execution uses NVIDIA Aerial/cuPHY and TensorRT \NRx; external AI is
Qwen2.5-1.5B prefill. The workload replay derives arrivals and token values from four non-overlapping
windows of the public BurstGPT trace~\cite{burstgpt}. The evaluated timing contract is the synthetic
period/deadline pair \texttt{P180/D155}; it is not labeled as a production HARQ deadline.

\paragraph{Qualification and experimental discipline.}
Each authoritative experiment freezes source hash, protocol, seeds, node assignment, bounds, exclusion
rules, and analyzer before opening outcomes. Development and holdout runs use different nodes and
seeds. A model prediction promoted to a physical boundary test is frozen before execution. Failed,
negative, and unqualified runs remain in the ledger. We report zero \emph{observed} violations rather
than infer zero probability or WCET from finite samples.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
The main safe comparison is \emph{certificate-preserving recovery-first}: it uses the same max-radio
decisions, initial all-fail radio admission, observable state, bounds, arrivals, exact offline weighted
selector, single-commit rule, and qualified workers, but physically drains unresolved recovery before
external AI. The comparison therefore isolates the value of AI-first atomic retiming; it is not a
test of whether recovery debt exists. MPS-only and debt-blind current-idle admission are diagnostic
unsafe baselines. The offline oracle observes the realized trace and supplies an upper bound; it is not
implementable.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
The anonymous artifact package contains the source snapshot, machine-readable protocols, manifest
hashes, analyzers, exact checker, independent certificate verifier, result summaries, and figure
builders needed to reproduce every aggregate from preserved raw records. Hardware campaigns require
the proprietary Aerial/cuPHY and TensorRT environment; model/checker, trace, and analysis paths run
without it. Node-specific names and absolute paths are removed from the review artifact.

\paragraph{Use of generative AI.}
Generative-AI tools assisted with language editing, document organization, bibliography normalization,
and consistency-checking scripts. The authors designed the system and model, fixed the experimental
protocols, executed the experiments, inspected failures, and verified every reported claim against
hashed artifacts. Generative AI produced no experimental measurement or ground-truth label.

\section{Evaluation}

\subsection{MPS is a mechanism, not the contract}

Under separate-client overrun, active-thread caps of 100\% and 20\% produced 2/1,500 and 1/1,500
deadline misses. A later lifecycle comparison observed 0/10,000 misses for a CPU socket sham and
12/10,000 for the GPU/MPS-client arm. These tests do not establish a universal failure probability.
They show that cap selection alone cannot be the certificate used by this paper.

\subsection{End-to-end variable-class qualification}

The authoritative warm \texttt{P180/D155} campaign used independent development and holdout nodes with
the same frozen source and different seeds. It admitted Qwen contexts from 16 to 512 tokens with class
bounds $\{35,35,35,40,65,75\}$\,ms.

Maximum observed \NRx, recovery, and radio-commit paths were 18.123, 13.465, and 118.022\,ms. Every

\subsection{Fault closure}

A finite-state audit checked 51 combinations of epoch, generation, fence, and marker state with no
invariant violation. The physical A0--A6 campaign covered clean operation, correlated \NRx failure,
reply delay before and after physical completion, stale and duplicate responses, terminal channel
fault, and continuation after fault.

Across qualified nodes it executed 700 epochs, 2,800 actual \NRx requests, 942 shared recoveries, and
2,800 radio commits with no observed deadline miss. It included 40 stale/duplicate replay pairs, four
terminal channel faults, and 260 post-fault continuation epochs. One other node violated the \NRx
whole-path bound and was retained as \UQ rather than pooled into the passing result.

\subsection{Predicting an unopened physical boundary}

The envelope was compared with exact feasibility on 16,023 qualified states and retrospectively with
1,200 physical decisions; both had zero mismatch. We then froze seven physical boundary cases before
execution. Every round ran four actual \NRx requests and used controlled suffix faults to set unresolved
debt, followed by real shared-cuPHY recovery and, when permitted, real Qwen.

Across two independent nodes and 180 rounds, model and runtime agreed in all 180. The run executed 720
\NRx requests, 330 recoveries, 90 Qwen units, and 720 commits with no observed deadline miss. At the
adjacent decision-time boundary, context-64 at conservative integer time 88\,ms was admitted in 30/30
rounds, while 89\,ms or later was rejected in 30/30.

\subsection{Certified scheduler scalability}

The polynomial candidate scheduler tries five deterministic list orders and returns the first schedule
accepted by an independent verifier. On 600 heterogeneous small states, exact search accepted 504 and
the certified scheduler accepted 494: false-safe was zero and false-conservative was ten. On the
qualified mode, decisions matched exact search on all 16,023 states.

For a 2,800-state grid with 1--64 debts and capacities 1/2/4/8, every returned certificate passed the
verifier. At 64 debts, candidate decision latency was 1.494\,ms p99 and 2.197\,ms maximum; verifier p99
was 0.131\,ms. These are finite-run CPU measurements.

\subsection{What the certificate prevents}

Two prespecified C162 cases isolate necessity from throughput. In E4, two recovery debts remain at
45\,ms and a context-256 AI request is visible. Mandatory-only recovery is feasible, but admitting the
AI transaction yields a bound-respecting completion of 165\,ms, 12\,ms past the 153\,ms radio guard.
In E6b, one debt remains and context-64 becomes unsafe at 89\,ms: the corresponding completion is
154\,ms, one millisecond past the guard. An idle-only debt-blind policy would accept both states; \sys
classified both as \QSN and rejected the AI launch in 30/30 physical rounds per case across two nodes.

These are contract-level counterexamples under Proposition~2. We do not label them observed
debt-blind deadline misses because the rejected kernels were not launched. Separate MPS-only diagnostics
did observe 2/1,500 and 1/1,500 misses at 100\% and 20\% active-thread caps, and a lifecycle experiment
observed 12/10,000 misses in its GPU/MPS arm versus 0/10,000 in the CPU sham. Together, the evidence
distinguishes an actual MPS failure mode from the precise conditional-debt admission that prevents a
qualified guarantee.

The counterexamples are relative to the qualified $B_{\mathrm{conv}}=25$\,ms service contract.
Holding all other charges fixed, E6b disappears at or below 24\,ms; E4 remains until 19\,ms, so only E4
survives for $19 < B_{\mathrm{conv}} \leq 24$\,ms. The authoritative Q2 recovery-path sample maximum
was 13.465\,ms (median 3.404\,ms), making 25\,ms 1.86$\times$ that sample maximum rather than
8$\times$. A sample maximum is not a replacement service bound: any lower $B_{\mathrm{conv}}$ defines
an unqualified counterfactual until the complete calendar, workload, placement, and lifecycle mode is
requalified. If 13.465\,ms were hypothetically promoted to a bound, both selected points would cease
to be witnesses. Proposition~2 still applies for every positive recovery charge: a tighter bound moves
the false-safe interval to a later decision time rather than making unresolved debt irrelevant, and
that new boundary requires prespecified physical validation.

\subsection{Strong baseline and no material headroom}

We compare \sys with the certificate-preserving recovery-first system described above. The only
semantic difference is
whether external AI may execute before unresolved recovery is physically completed. On four
non-overlapping BurstGPT calibration windows containing 4,290 requests and 1,129,504 offered tokens,
both systems assigned 931 timely requests and 385,262 tokens. Under a sensitivity charging every
recovery its full 25\,ms bound, \sys improved timely token value by only 0.066\%, far below the
prespecified 5\% minimum effect. We therefore did not open a confirmatory throughput holdout.

This negative result narrows the contribution. Conditional capacity exists and changes admission, but
the evaluated 1\,s AI SLO lets recovery-first service catch up. \sys is not presented as a
higher-throughput optimizer for this trace.

\subsection{Production exit gates}

A source audit found a stronger intermediate target than \texttt{P180/D155}. Aerial testMAC configures
CRC-bearing UL indications at $T0+4.5$\,ms; its validator reconstructs slot $T0$ and classifies later
handler arrival as late. NVIDIA describes testMAC as a developer L2 for a controlled environment, so
this is a vendor integration target rather than a field-DU \texttt{d\_MAC}.

Under optimistic clean, warm, no-AI conditions, local wait-then-recover and same-GPU speculative paths
completed 0/1,000 requests within 4.5\,ms. Moving precomputed \NRx inputs to GPU1 also completed
0/1,000. Moving channel estimation through CRC behind raw-IQ P2P improved completion to 852/1,000
(3.831\,ms median), but retained 148 late samples; disabling Python GC did not remove the tail.
Binding cuPHY and caller-owned TensorRT to one stream improved timely completion to 885/1,000 but
retained 115 late samples. A diagnostic 300-request stage profile localized the tail to LS channel
estimation (0.866/4.873/9.592\,ms p50/p99/max, 0.9603 correlation with pair wall time), while the
TensorRT graph had a 0.886\,ms p99 and forward P2P copy a 69.3\,$\mu$s p99. Profiling changes timing,
so these values are mechanism evidence rather than qualification. The envelope still classifies every
tested implementation as unqualified at this threshold. These diagnostics are neither WCET evidence
nor a production trace.

The Aerial TDL-A subdomain remains unqualified. Across raw and normalized Aerial TDL-A,
public-reference DMRS/MCS/start-symbol settings, one-antenna frequency/time channel execution, and an
FP32 engine built as in the public notebook, conventional decoding succeeded in every ten-TB arm while
\NRx succeeded in none. This localizes the gap to an unsupported model/channel interface or domain
rather than a simple power, radio-profile, transmitter, or precision setting.

We next reconstructed the public Sionna 1.0.2/TensorFlow 2.19 contract. Clean MCS7 and default
Rayleigh controls passed, and a fixed development matrix identified CDL-D/E at 100\,ns as holdout
candidates while preserving A/B/C failures. Before opening new payload, slot, and channel seeds, we
froze a D/E paired holdout with five Es/No strata and 50 blocks per stratum per model. Both pipelines
passed 50/50 at 10\,dB. Across low-SNR strata, \NRx-only correctness was 31 versus 12
conventional-only (two-sided exact $p=0.00540$). This closes the channel gate only for the declared
finite-sample Sionna CDL-D/E mode, not TDL-A, field IQ, or production timing.

\paragraph{AI-and-RAN resource sharing.}
YinYangRAN adjusts MPS fractions between PHY and ML and can use CPU fallback~\cite{yinyangran}.
CloudRIC places RAN requests across heterogeneous accelerators using queue and deadline
estimates~\cite{cloudric}. Interplay and CAORA use learning-based orchestration and MIG
allocation~\cite{interplay,caora}; HAF separates slow placement from fast deadline-aware CPU/GPU
allocation~\cite{haf}. A distributed AI-RAN platform addresses placement across RAN
sites~\cite{distributedairan}. These systems establish shared AI/RAN acceleration, dynamic allocation,
fallback, and deadline-aware placement as prior art. \sys addresses a narrower state: conventional
recovery of the same TB remains a future mandatory obligation until optional \NRx resolves.

\paragraph{Slack reclamation and bounded background work.}
Concordia reserves CPU capacity for vRAN and reclaims the remainder~\cite{concordia}. DARIS combines MPS,
streams, and stage boundaries to admit lower-priority DNN work~\cite{daris}. Real-time GPU systems such
as REEF, XSched, and nvtaskset provide preemption, isolation, or context-scheduling
mechanisms~\cite{reef,xsched,nvtaskset}. \sys uses rather than claims these principles; its certificate
also includes radio recovery semantics, endpoint/transport ownership, and physical completion.

\paragraph{AI/conventional PHY paths.}
ARCHES selects neural or conventional PHY experts by channel conditions~\cite{arches}. OCUDU exposes
in-DU AI-RAN timing classes, retains a conventional path, and validates result and lifecycle
behavior~\cite{ocudu}. These works prevent us from claiming novelty for neural/conventional coexistence
or fallback. In the public designs reviewed through September 2026, we did not find the combination of
a multi-cell all-fail recovery calendar, atomic external-AI leasing, and a provenance-qualified
predictive envelope. This is a scoped literature finding, not proof that no unpublished or differently
named work exists.

\paragraph{MEC scheduling.}
SMEC coordinates application SLOs through decoupled RAN and edge schedulers and uses MPS priority at
the MEC GPU~\cite{smec}. Its AI workloads execute on a separate edge server rather than sharing the PHY
GPU with optional same-TB recovery. We do not claim novelty for 5G-aware deadline scheduling, only for
the conditional recovery contract evaluated here.

\section{Limitations}

First, \texttt{P180/D155} is a synthetic harness contract: $P$ is the harness inter-release period,
not an NR slot duration, and we do not map \texttt{D155} to a production HARQ deadline. The C163
production \texttt{d\_MAC} trace builder, clock and provenance checks, request-specific exact/certified
bridge, and expiry validator are implemented and pass 24 unit tests, but we lack a target DU/FAPI
trace. Once such a trace is available, this path can classify request-specific expiries with the same
contract semantics; the resulting timing vector and mode must still be physically requalified. Aerial
testMAC exposes a $T0+4.5$\,ms UL-indication threshold, but it is a controlled developer L2 rather than
the target MAC. Every implementation tested retained violations at that threshold; even the best
raw-IQ two-GPU prototype was timely for only 885/1,000 clean requests. We therefore make no production
HARQ guarantee.

Second, qualified bounds are finite-sample whole-path bounds. Zero observed violations do not prove a
WCET or zero failure probability. A different hardware, software, placement, or lifecycle fingerprint
must be requalified.

Third, all authoritative hardware results use the A100 family. Four-GPU and multi-home paths exercise
NVLink P2P, but cross-family validity is untested.

Fourth, the fault model excludes arbitrary GPU/driver hang and full process-replacement recovery. Five
lifecycle modes remain \UQ, and a separate long-tail observation has an unresolved root cause.

Fifth, PHY generalization is limited. External Aerial TDL-A development screens retained conventional
success on every ten-TB arm and \NRx success on none after testing normalization, public-reference
radio parameters, exact one-antenna transmission, frequency/time channel execution, and the public
notebook's FP32 engine precision. Separately, a frozen Sionna CDL-D/E holdout passed its high-SNR
pipeline and conditional-value gates over 500 paired blocks, with 31 \NRx-only and 12
conventional-only low-SNR outcomes. This qualifies that exact standardized simulated-channel mode
only. It does not establish Aerial TDL-A, recorded field-IQ, or over-the-air generalization, and the
D/E mode has not traversed production timing and integrated service-vector qualification.

Finally, the strongest throughput comparison found no material advantage. A workload with tighter,
independently justified AI deadlines may expose a benefit, but selecting such a deadline after seeing
the current result would be post hoc. The demonstrated operational value is advance classification of
safe, no-slack, infeasible, and unqualified modes, plus launch-time rejection of certificate-breaking
work. This paper therefore makes a substrate and envelope claim.

\section{Conclusion}

Optional \NRx changes shared-GPU scheduling because its unresolved conventional path is future mandatory
work. \sys makes that work explicit as recovery debt, protects it with an executable all-fail
certificate, and admits external AI only through a transaction whose control, transport, and physical
completion preserve the certificate. Its feasibility envelope predicts when a qualified mode is
safe-and-useful, safe-without-slack, mandatory-infeasible, or unqualified.

The evaluated A100 modes show that this contract can drive actual \NRx, shared cuPHY recovery, and
Qwen execution across multiple GPUs and fault transitions. The evaluation also states where the claim
ends: MPS is not isolation, finite samples are not WCET, production timing is absent, and the strong
baseline leaves no material throughput headroom. These boundaries are part of the result.
