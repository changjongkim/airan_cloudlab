\keywords{AI-RAN, GPU scheduling, neural receiver, conditional recovery, MPS, admission control}

\begin{abstract}
As AI-RAN platforms share GPUs between radio processing and AI inference, neural receivers introduce
work that GPU schedulers do not track. Until its neural receiver (\NRx) completes, an uplink transport
block (TB) has a pending recovery: a conventional decoding that is absent from the GPU queue but
mandatory if the receiver fails. This paper presents \sys, a multi-GPU scheduler that tracks
these pending recoveries explicitly. \sys admits speculative receivers, retimes recovery, and grants GPU time to external
AI only when an independent verifier confirms that every unresolved TB can still recover if all of them
fail. \sys keeps each accepted decision valid through CUDA launch, inter-GPU transport, and physical completion
with bounded revalidation, latest-start enforcement, and freeing of GPU time only after confirmed completion. On two four-GPU A100
nodes under a qualified synthetic timing contract, \sys records 0/120 safety violations in physical
boundary diagnostics, where idle-time admission records 40/40. Across 13,716 feasible capacity-model
points, \sys admits more AI capacity than static reservation at 26.2\%, and its p99 decision time is
1.494~ms at 64 pending recoveries.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

As radio access networks (RANs) move baseband processing onto GPUs, AI-RAN platforms run radio
physical-layer (PHY) processing and AI inference on the same accelerators
~\cite{aerial,yinyangran,interplay,distributedairan}. To use the GPU capacity that radio processing
leaves idle, these platforms admit AI services, such as large language model (LLM) inference,
alongside deadline-critical PHY tasks~\cite{caora,haf}. This co-location is effective only when every
radio task still completes before its processing deadline~\cite{concordia}, and conventional GPU
sharing can disrupt 5G processing~\cite{yinyangran}. Neural receivers make this requirement harder to
enforce. A neural receiver replaces channel estimation, equalization, and demapping with a learned
model whose decoding outcome is known only after it executes~\cite{deeprx,nrx5g}. However, the GPU
schedulers of these platforms protect only the radio work that is already visible in a GPU queue or in
a resource allocation.

The scheduling challenge stems from the fallback structure of neural reception. A neural receiver
(\NRx) is optional work: a timely success delivers the uplink transport block (TB), while a failure, a
late result, or a lost result makes the conventional receiver mandatory for the same TB. Until the
\NRx outcome resolves, this conventional recovery is absent from the GPU queue, but it must remain
executable before the radio deadline. When several cells run \NRx together, their recoveries can
contend for one conventional decoder, and their failures can be correlated. In addition, a
Multi-Process Service (MPS) share limits the threads of a co-running process, but it does not bound
host scheduling, queueing, or process lifecycle~\cite{nvidiampS}. Thus, a scheduler that admits AI
into apparently idle GPU time can push a future recovery past its deadline, and a scheduler that
reserves every possible recovery leaves the capacity released by \NRx success unused.

To share accelerators between radio processing and other workloads, existing systems employ different
approaches. As summarized in Table~\ref{tab:closest}, these systems differ in the object that their
schedulers protect or allocate. vRAN and AI-RAN resource managers
~\cite{yinyangran,cloudric,concordia,interplay,caora,haf,distributedairan} allocate compute fractions,
queued requests, reserved capacity, or worker placement between RAN functions and other workloads. GPU
scheduling systems~\cite{daris,reef,xsched,nvtaskset} partition, prioritize, or preempt GPU stages,
kernels, and contexts. PHY and edge platforms~\cite{arches,ocudu,smec} select PHY models, expose timing
classes inside the distributed unit (DU), or coordinate edge service-level objectives (SLOs). However,
their scheduling units are resources or queued jobs. Their public designs neither represent the
recovery that a speculative receiver may create nor keep an admission decision valid through GPU
launch and completion, where a decision that is valid when made can become invalid before it executes.

In this paper, we present \sys, a recovery-safe multi-GPU scheduler that shares AI-RAN GPUs
between neural receivers and AI inference.
\sys tracks each unresolved recovery as a \emph{pending recovery} and accepts a scheduling decision
only when an independent verifier confirms that every unresolved TB can still recover if all of them
fail. Specifically, \sys introduces 1) a recovery-aware scheduling model that jointly decides \NRx
admission, recovery timing, and external-AI slots over one set of pending recoveries shared by several cuPHY
instances, 2) schedule enforcement that keeps each accepted decision valid through
CUDA launch, IPC/P2P transport, and physical completion with bounded revalidation, absolute
latest-start enforcement, atomic outcome batches, and freeing of GPU time only after confirmed completion, and 3) an admission limit, qualified by the measurement conditions of every bound, that classifies each state
as \QSU, \QSN, \MI, or \UQ before execution. Our
evaluation on two four-GPU A100 nodes under a qualified synthetic timing contract shows that physical
boundary diagnostics record 0/120 safety violations for \sys and 40/40 for idle-time admission. Under
bursty BurstGPT-based arrivals with a 200~ms AI SLO, \sys serves 6.2-18.5\% more on-time AI tokens than
its recovery-first ablation and static reservation serves none, while idle-time admission misses the
recovery deadline in up to 10.0\% of periods. On one GPU, \sys makes the same decisions, and early AI
slots that share the GPU with \NRx serve 2.6-70.3\% more on-time AI tokens without losing a timely \NRx
result. In
1,200 physical release periods, the shared lane finishes every period before its scheduled end, and under seven injected
fault classes every TB commits exactly once before expiry. Across
13,716 feasible capacity-model points, \sys admits more AI capacity than static reservation at 3,600
(26.2\%). The scheduler matches an exact checker on 16,023 states, and its p99 decision time is
1.494~ms at 64 pending recoveries. In the original trace mode, \sys and recovery-first
deliver the same 349,387 timely tokens.

\section{Background and Motivation}

\subsection{AI-RAN on shared GPUs}

AI-RAN platforms execute RAN functions and AI services on the same GPUs
~\cite{aerial,yinyangran,cloudric,interplay,caora,haf}. In our setting, these GPUs serve three classes
of work: optional neural reception, mandatory conventional reception, and external AI.

\noindent\textbf{Neural Reception.}
An uplink TB is decoded by a conventional receiver, such as the cuPHY physical uplink shared channel
(PUSCH) pipeline~\cite{aerial}, or by a neural receiver~\cite{deeprx,nrx5g}. A cuPHY instance runs the
PUSCH pipelines of several cells, and in our evaluated contract each cell sends one TB per release period.
In this contract (Figure~\ref{fig:airan-background}), a TB commits its \NRx result when the
result passes its cyclic redundancy check (CRC) by the \NRx deadline; otherwise, one conventional
recovery must complete before the recovery deadline. Figure~\ref{fig:debt}a shows that this outcome is uncertain. Across a synthetic fading channel without a direct path (block Rayleigh) with 500 TBs per point and two 3GPP channels with a direct path, the CDL-D and CDL-E
models~\cite{tr38901,sionna} with 50 TBs per point, the \NRx success rate rises from near 0\% to near
100\% within 1~dB of the point at which the conventional receiver decodes half of the TBs. In CDL-D and
CDL-E, the conventional receiver also decodes TBs that \NRx misses. The channel quality indicator that
drives link adaptation targets a 10\% transport block error probability~\cite{ts38214}, which keeps TBs
near this transition.

\noindent\textbf{GPU Sharing.}
MPS allows CUDA processes to execute concurrently and limits the active-thread percentage of each
client~\cite{nvidiampS}, but it does not bound host scheduling, queueing, context lifecycle, or
allocation. Multi-Instance GPU (MIG)
partitions provide spatial isolation, but a fixed partition cannot reclaim the capacity that an \NRx
success releases. Figure~\ref{fig:execution}a co-runs Qwen2.5-1.5B prefill~\cite{qwen25} with a
two-cell 273-PRB PUSCH pipeline and sweeps the MPS share of Qwen. A single 16-token request keeps the
radio p99 latency within 11.9~ms at every share, against 8.9-10.0~ms alone. Eight 512-token requests
keep it within 13.9~ms up to an 80\% share but reach 21.2-22.9~ms at 100\%, where 93 of 1,000 slots
miss the 21~ms deadline. Thus, an MPS share is safe only for the AI class under which it is qualified.

\noindent\textbf{Execution Tails.}
Figure~\ref{fig:execution}b normalizes each latency by its median. The \NRx TensorRT graph stays within
3.5\% of its median, while end-to-end paths do not. At the 4.5~ms indication deadline of the Aerial
testMAC~\cite{aerial}, cross-GPU receiver pairs reach 4.0 and 3.7 times their median in a Python path
and a partly native C++/CUDA path. Host construction of a schedule reaches 29 times its
median. Thus, a schedule computed from kernel execution times can become invalid before it executes.

These properties couple radio correctness to GPU admission decisions that precede the receiver outcome.
While existing systems allocate GPU resources between RAN and AI workloads
~\cite{yinyangran,cloudric,interplay,caora,haf} or preempt GPU kernels~\cite{reef,xsched,daris}, their
public designs do not schedule the conditional recovery that neural reception creates.

\subsection{Challenges in conditional recovery scheduling}
\label{sec:requirements}

Existing schedulers admit AI against the work in GPU queues or against a fixed reservation
~\cite{yinyangran,cloudric,concordia}. For neural reception, this admission exposes three challenges:
uncertain recovery need, one recovery lane shared by the cells of several cuPHY instances, and decisions that must remain
valid through execution.

\noindent\textbf{Pending Recovery.}
Before the \NRx outcome resolves, the conventional recovery of a TB is conditional in the observed
execution and mandatory in the failure branch. We call it a \emph{pending recovery}.
Figure~\ref{fig:debt}b converts a per-TB failure probability $p$ into the number of recoveries that a
release period with four admitted TBs requires; measured periods follow the curves for independent
failures. Idle-time admission reserves no recovery and is wrong whenever one recovery is required,
which occurs in 34\% of periods at $p=0.1$. Static reservation holds four recoveries, which a period
uses only when all four TBs fail, once in $10^4$ periods under independent failures. A within-period
failure correlation of 0.1 or 0.3 makes this all-four event 16 or 116 times more likely. Thus,
planning for the typical number of recoveries is unsafe, and holding every reservation until all
outcomes resolve leaves released capacity unused.

\noindent\textbf{Scheduling Failures.}
Figure~\ref{fig:scheduling-problem} shows three scheduling failures after two timely successes in our
evaluated contract. Idle-time admission sees an empty queue at the 45~ms \NRx deadline, admits a
256-token AI request, and pushes a required recovery past the recovery deadline. Static reservation keeps the reservations of
the two successful TBs and rejects a 128-token AI request that fits after they are released. Per-instance
schedules are each valid for one cuPHY instance, but they claim the same interval on the shared
recovery lane. Thus, a scheduler must represent unresolved recovery explicitly, recompute the schedule
at each outcome event, and verify one schedule for the global set of pending recoveries on the contended lane.

\noindent\textbf{Execution Gaps.}
A feasible schedule must remain feasible when it executes. Table~\ref{tab:counterexamples} lists seven
counterexamples that define this requirement. In our development runs, a 23.5~ms host stall launched a
128-token AI request outside its scheduled interval, and applying simultaneous outcomes in sequence created
an intermediate state that never existed. Thus, a scheduler must charge launch control in every schedule, apply
simultaneous outcomes atomically, and bind ownership to physical completion. This motivates how \sys enforces its schedules in
Section~\ref{sec:design}.

\section{Backstop Design}
\label{sec:design}

\sys schedules pending recovery as explicit state and keeps each scheduling decision valid until its GPU
effect completes. This section presents the architecture, pending-recovery scheduling,
schedule enforcement on the GPU, and the event algorithm, and then states the guarantee scope and the
implementation.

\subsection{Overall architecture}
\label{sec:design-overall}

\sys schedules pending recovery and external AI on the GPUs of one AI-RAN node.
Figure~\ref{fig:overall} shows the architecture of \sys, which separates a control layer on the host from
an execution layer of persistent MPS workers, and follows one release period through seven steps
(\ding{182}-\ding{188}). The figure uses the evaluated example in which TB1 and TB2 succeed, TB3 fails
its CRC, TB4 is late, and TB5 is rejected at admission.

\noindent\textbf{Control Layer.}
One host coordinator holds four control objects. \textit{Decision Pages} record, for each TB, the \NRx
outcome, the resulting action, and the version of the decision. \textit{Pending Recoveries} lists every
TB whose conventional recovery is still required, with its cuPHY instance, its recovery window, and its
recovery bound $c$; in the example, TB3 and TB4 remain with the 45-153~ms window and $c=25$~ms, while the
entries of TB1 and TB2 are cancelled. \textit{Recovery-Lane Schedule} holds the current schedule $X_t$
of the shared recovery lane and a candidate $X'$ that adds one AI slot with its 5~ms launch budget before the recovery deadline. \textit{Independent Verifier} commits $X'$ or keeps $X_t$.

\noindent\textbf{Execution Layer.}
Persistent MPS workers execute all GPU work. In the multi-GPU placement (Figure~\ref{fig:overall}b),
GPU0 and GPU1 run the cuPHY instances that own TB1-TB3 and TB4-TB5 at a 50\% MPS share. GPU3 runs the
TensorRT FP16 \NRx worker as a CUDA graph at 80\%, receives IQ samples and channel estimates over NVLink
P2P, and returns log-likelihood ratios (LLRs). GPU2 runs the shared cuPHY recovery lane at 80\% and the
Qwen worker at 20\% behind a latest-start gate, and it writes the completion markers. In the single-GPU
placement (Figure~\ref{fig:overall}a), the same processes share one device and exchange IQ, LLR, and TB
buffers through CUDA IPC rings.

\noindent\textbf{Scheduling Loop.}
Each cuPHY instance publishes the \NRx outcome of its TBs to a decision page (\ding{182}). At the
\NRx deadline, one outcome batch updates the pending-recovery table (\ding{183}). The candidate scheduler
retimes the required recoveries around a candidate AI slot (\ding{184}), and the independent verifier
checks the all-fail schedule, credits, and version before it commits $X'$ or keeps $X_t$
(\ding{185}). The coordinator then publishes each action with its version (\ding{186}) and sends the AI
slot with an absolute latest-start to the Qwen worker (\ding{187}). A completion or non-launch marker
frees the slot (\ding{188}), and the shared lane runs the required recoveries in the committed order.

\noindent\textbf{Scheduling Objects.}
The scheduler uses only the information that is visible at the current event. A radio job and an
external-AI job are
\begin{equation}
 J_i=(r_i,d_i,h_i,c_i,K_i,q_i), \qquad
 A_a=(u_a,v_a,\ell_a,b_a,g_a),
\end{equation}
where $r_i$ and $d_i$ are the release and expiry, $h_i$ is the owning cuPHY instance, $c_i$ is the
conventional host-to-commit bound, $K_i$ contains the eligible \NRx endpoints, and $q_i$ is the optional
radio value; $u_a$, $v_a$, $\ell_a$, $b_a$, and $g_a$ are the AI arrival, timely value, deadline,
physical-completion bound, and recovery margin. Decisions cannot use future CRC outcomes, future arrivals,
realized service times, or hidden channel labels.

\noindent\textbf{Qualified Modes.}
A service bound is valid for scheduling only under matching measurement conditions
\begin{equation}
 \pi=(\text{GPU},\text{placement},\text{MPS},\text{lifecycle},\text{co-run},\text{software}).
\end{equation}
Therefore, a warm local measurement cannot qualify a cold, restarted, or remote path. The scheduler marks
a state \UQ whenever a required bound was not measured under the active conditions, even when a sample finishes before its
deadline.

\noindent\textbf{Separation of Proposal and Authority.}
The candidate scheduler may use a fast list heuristic and may reject a feasible placement. It cannot
create an unsafe execution because only the verifier can commit a candidate. With this separation,
the evaluation compares decision quality with an exact oracle while safety remains independent of the
heuristic.

\subsection{Pending-recovery scheduling}
\label{sec:design-recovery}

An unresolved \NRx creates required future work even when no conventional kernel is queued. \sys records
this work as a pending recovery
\begin{equation}
 R_i=(a_i(t),d_i-g_i,c_i,h_i),
\end{equation}
where $a_i(t)$ is its earliest legal start. For the unresolved set $U(t)$, an all-fail schedule
assigns every $R_i$ a non-overlapping interval $[s_i,s_i+c_i]$ on its physical recovery lane, after
release and before $d_i-g_i$. Contending transport and AI busy intervals belong to the same
schedule.

\noindent\textbf{All-Fail Dominance.}
\sys needs one schedule, not one schedule per combination of outcomes.
\begin{proposition}[All-fail dominance]
If a timely \NRx success only deletes its conventional recovery and does not increase any remaining
job's demand, a feasible all-fail schedule for $U(t)$ is feasible for every failure subset
$F\subseteq U(t)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Delete successful requests from the all-fail schedule. Release, expiry, capacity, and non-overlap
constraints of every remaining interval remain unchanged.
\end{proof}
The reduction replaces $2^{|U|}$ outcome schedules with one executable schedule. It does not apply when a
success creates new mandatory work or changes another service bound; that case requires a new mode.

\noindent\textbf{Admission and Outcome Batch.}
Figure~\ref{fig:atomic-replan}a shows one transaction on the shared lane. At release, admission reserves
one pending recovery per TB before any optional dispatch (\ding{182}). TB1-TB4 fit before the recovery deadline, and
TB5 is rejected because its reservation would cross the recovery deadline. Therefore, endpoint rejection never removes
the conventional path. At the 45~ms \NRx deadline, one outcome batch applies all observed outcomes as one atomic
update (\ding{183}): TB1 and TB2 succeed and cancel their pending recoveries, while TB3 and TB4 fail
and keep theirs. The capacity that TB1 and TB2 release stays free until the recovery deadline, and the committed schedule
$X_t$ holds two recoveries. Sequential replay of the same outcomes is invalid because it can create
intermediate states that never coexisted.

\noindent\textbf{Candidate AI Slot.}
Figure~\ref{fig:atomic-replan}b adds one AI slot to $X_t$. For a 128-token request, the candidate $X'$
places the 5~ms launch budget and the AI slot first and retimes TB3 and TB4 behind them
(\ding{184}). The last recovery still ends before the recovery deadline, and the verifier commits $X'$ as version
$g+1$ (\ding{185}). For a 256-token request, TB4 would end after the recovery deadline; the verifier rejects $X'$ and
keeps $X_t$.

\noindent\textbf{Early AI Slot.}
The recovery lane stays idle from release until the \NRx deadline, when the first recovery can start.
Therefore, \sys also runs the same check at release: an \emph{early AI slot} starts at release, and the
verifier commits it only when the all-fail schedule of every admitted TB, with the slot as an AI busy
interval, still ends before the recovery deadline. In the evaluated contract, four recoveries take
100~ms of the 108~ms recovery window, and an early slot may end up to 8~ms after the \NRx deadline.
This limit admits a 16-128-token request and rejects a 256- or 512-token request. On one GPU,
the early slot shares the GPU with \NRx. A \NRx result that the slot delays past the \NRx deadline only
becomes a required recovery, which the all-fail schedule already holds. Thus, an early slot can cost
\NRx value but not radio safety.

\noindent\textbf{Global Atomic State.}
Schedule scope follows the contended lane: disjoint lanes compose by union, while cuPHY instances that
share a recovery lane require one global set of pending recoveries. The complete runtime state is
\begin{equation}
 X_t=(S_t,Q_t,T_t,L_t,E_t,C_t,G_t),
\end{equation}
where $S$ is the recovery schedule, $Q$ and $T$ are the endpoint and transport credits, $L$ holds the AI
slots, $E$ holds the completion events and worker epochs, $C$ is the radio commit state, and $G$ is the
multi-instance AI ownership. The verifier commits a private $X'$ only when
\begin{align}
\mathrm{VALID}(X') \equiv {}& \mathrm{ExecutableRecovery}(S') \land
 \mathrm{UniqueCredits}(Q',T') \nonumber\\
 &\land\ \mathrm{TimelyAI}(L',E') \land \mathrm{CurrentVersions}(X').
\end{align}
A rejected candidate leaves $X_t$ intact, which eliminates partial calendar updates and orphan AI slots.

\noindent\textbf{Safety Invariant.}
The verifier and the enforcement in Section~\ref{sec:design-refinement} maintain one invariant across
all events.
\begin{invariant}[Recovery, ownership, and single commit]
For every admitted radio request, every allowed unresolved-outcome branch retains a collision-free
conventional path completing by expiry. No endpoint, transport, AI, or output credit is reused before
matching terminal physical evidence, and each request commits at most one radio result.
\end{invariant}

\noindent\textbf{Bounded Launch.}
The coordinator commits $L$ as version $g+1$ (\ding{182}) and sends $\mathrm{run}(L,\text{latest-start})$
to the Qwen worker (\ding{183}). Prepare creates one launch ticket that carries the calendar
version, worker epoch, ring and transport credit, and absolute latest-start. The worker launches only
when the current time is at or before the latest-start (\ding{184}); it then writes the launch marker
\texttt{L.launch} and submits the prefill kernels to the GPU2 stream. Otherwise, it replies with a
non-launch marker and does not execute the stale schedule.

\noindent\textbf{Physical Retirement.}
When the CUDA event completes, the worker writes the completion marker \texttt{L.complete} and replies
(\ding{185}). The coordinator frees $L$ only on a marker that matches its version and advances to
version $g+2$ (\ding{186}). RPC completion is not GPU completion. Therefore, slots and credits remain
owned until a matching CUDA completion event, and a transport-copy completion cannot replace a receiver or recovery
completion.

\noindent\textbf{Fault Handling.}
The red steps show the injected faults. When the reply is delayed or lost after GPU completion, the
coordinator reads \texttt{L.complete} and frees $L$ from the marker (\textcolor{dred}{\ding{182}}).
When the channel is lost before GPU completion, only \texttt{L.launch} exists at the end of the slot; the
coordinator keeps $L$ owned, stops AI admission, and continues radio recovery
(\textcolor{dred}{\ding{183}}). A replay of the nrx-17 outcome batch or of a period-16 batch leaves the
radio state unchanged (\textcolor{dred}{\ding{184}}), and a second response for TB4 of period 17
produces no commit because the commit set holds one entry per TB (\textcolor{dred}{\ding{185}}). A
timeout without physical evidence invalidates the version and closes new optional admission.

\noindent\textbf{Distributed and Transport Ownership.}
One global AI queue can serve several cuPHY instances, but each instance holds at most one pending
launch ticket in an ownership epoch, and commit transfers launch authority only while the local and global
schedules remain current. CUDA IPC and NVLink P2P avoid a host-staging copy and also make ownership
explicit: each ring entry has one version, one physical completion state, and one scheduler-visible completion event. Therefore, \sys charges transport inside the qualified service path. Same-worker
reconnect carries the version through bounded markers, while full process replacement without durable
reconciliation remains \UQ.

\subsection{Event-scheduling algorithm}
\label{sec:design-algorithm}

\sys is an event-driven scheduling algorithm with a verified commit point. Algorithm~1 runs at every
event batch and rejects optional work whenever measurement conditions, recovery feasibility, or ownership cannot be
proven.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\fbox{\begin{minipage}{0.94\linewidth}
\small
\textbf{Algorithm 1: \textsc{ScheduleEvent}$(X_t,\mathcal{E}_t,\mathcal{A}_t,\pi)$}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item If any required bound disagrees with $\pi$, return \UQ and disable new optional work.
  \item Apply the event batch $\mathcal{E}_t$ atomically: add a pending recovery for each new radio job;
        cancel it only for a timely success; keep it for failure, lateness, or missing evidence.
  \item Place every required recovery on its qualified physical lane. If no all-fail calendar exists,
        return \MI and reject new \NRx and AI admission.
  \item Visit only visible AI jobs in deterministic policy order. For each job, construct $X'$ by
        jointly retiming recovery and adding one complete AI slot.
  \item If the independent verifier accepts $X'$, atomically commit it, dispatch its first action,
        and return \QSU. Otherwise discard $X'$ without changing $X_t$.
  \item If no AI candidate passes, commit the recovery-only calendar and return \QSN. A matching
        terminal event ends ownership and invokes \textsc{ScheduleEvent} again.
\end{enumerate}
\end{minipage}}

\noindent\textbf{Event Processing.}
Step~1 disables new optional work and returns \UQ when a required bound disagrees with the active
measurement conditions $\pi$. Step~2 applies the event batch atomically: it adds a pending recovery for each new radio
job, cancels a pending recovery only for a timely success, and keeps it for failure, lateness, or missing
evidence. Step~3 places every required recovery on its qualified lane and returns \MI when no all-fail
calendar exists. Steps~4 and~5 visit the visible AI jobs in a deterministic policy order, build one
candidate $X'$ per job, and commit the first candidate that the verifier accepts, which returns \QSU.
Step~6 commits the recovery-only calendar and returns \QSN when no AI candidate passes.

\noindent\textbf{Decision Window.}
For $m_h$ equal-length recoveries compacted at expiry $d_h$ with margin $g_h$, let $b_{\mathrm{eff}}$
include synchronous control, AI service, and the completion margin. AI-first execution is safe under the
sufficient, mode-specific condition
\begin{equation}
 T_{\mathrm{dec},h}+b_{\mathrm{eff}}
 \le d_h-g_h-m_hc_h. \label{eq:window}
\end{equation}
The right side is the latest time at which the first required recovery can start. Therefore, the inequality charges
decision and launch delay and does not assume immediate dispatch.

\noindent\textbf{Idle Time.}
The same window shows why idle GPU time cannot authorize admission.
\begin{proposition}[Idle time is insufficient]
\label{prop:idletime}
On one non-preemptive lane with common recovery deadline $D$, unresolved bounds $c_1,\ldots,c_m$, and AI bound
$b_{\mathrm{eff}}$, suppose mandatory-only execution is feasible but
\begin{equation}
  t+b_{\mathrm{eff}}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_i>D. \label{eq:idletime}
\end{equation}
Then admitting AI into idle time while ignoring pending recovery cannot guarantee the radio contract.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose the allowed realization in which AI and every recovery take their declared bounds. The final
recovery completes after $D$ by Equation~\ref{eq:idletime}; present idle time does not erase future
mandatory demand.
\end{proof}

\noindent\textbf{Safe Reclaimable Capacity.}
The same invariant predicts the capacity that the algorithm can expose. Let $W$ be the window up to the recovery deadline, $L$
the AI deadline, $a$ one complete AI transaction, $c$ one recovery bound, $M_g$ the pending recoveries
that static reservation holds, and $F_g$ the required recoveries on lane $g$. With $[x]_+=\max(0,x)$,
\begin{align}
 K_{\mathrm{static}}&=\sum_g\left\lfloor\frac{[\min(L,W-M_gc)]_+}{a}\right\rfloor,\\
 K_{\mathrm{RF}}&=\sum_g\left\lfloor\frac{[\min(L,W)-F_gc]_+}{a}\right\rfloor,\\
 K_{\text{\sys}}&=\sum_g\left\lfloor\frac{[\min(L,W-F_gc)]_+}{a}\right\rfloor. \label{eq:capacity}
\end{align}
Static reservation holds every pending recovery, recovery-first executes the required recoveries before
AI, and \sys may place AI first while keeping $F_gc$ before the recovery deadline. Multi-class BurstGPT batches use
exact subset, lane, order, and recovery-insertion enumeration. Failure probabilities predict expected
value, while the all-fail safety check does not depend on the failure distribution.

Table~\ref{tab:classes} lists the four outcomes of a decision. They keep scheduling failure distinct from

\subsection{Guarantee scope}
\label{sec:guarantee-scope}

\sys provides a conditional guarantee for an explicitly qualified mode. Qualification binds service
bounds to the environment in which they were measured and defines the events that the runtime must
contain.

\noindent\textbf{Mode Conditions.}
A mode fixes GPU topology, MPS settings, process lifecycle, worker epoch, memory residency, co-run class,
software, and finite-sample whole-path bounds. Changing any element requires requalification. The
synthetic release period and result expiry used in evaluation are harness parameters, not NR slot or
production HARQ timing.

\noindent\textbf{Contained Events.}
The runtime handles \NRx failure and lateness, stale and duplicate messages, bounded reply loss before or
after a control-plane effect, missing AI replies after physical completion, and same-worker channel
reconnection. It stops new AI admission when completion evidence is absent.

\noindent\textbf{Excluded Events.}
Arbitrary GPU hangs, driver reset, unbounded host suspension, production-clock uncertainty, and
unqualified workloads remain outside the claim. Mandatory offered load without an all-fail schedule is
\MI rather than a reason to weaken the safety check.

\subsection{Backstop implementation}
\label{sec:implementation}

\sys binds the model to persistent CUDA workers and explicit control objects, which makes schedule
state observable and enforceable across process and GPU boundaries.

\noindent\textbf{Radio and AI Workers.}
MIG is disabled and MPS is enabled. cuPHY instances, \NRx endpoints, conventional recovery, and external
AI run in separate persistent workers. TensorRT \NRx workers hold preallocated bindings and CUDA graphs,
persistent Aerial cuPHY workers execute conventional recovery, and a Qwen2.5-1.5B worker executes bounded
prefill classes. In the evaluated placement (Figure~\ref{fig:overall}b), GPU0 and GPU1 host the cuPHY
instances with a 50\% MPS thread limit, GPU3 hosts the \NRx endpoint, and GPU2 hosts the shared cuPHY
lane and the Qwen worker, which runs as a low-priority MPS client with a 20\% thread limit. In the
single-GPU placement (Figure~\ref{fig:overall}a), the same workers share one GPU with the same MPS
limits. One cuPHY receiver of the harness holds 8.6~GiB, and the recovery lane runs one recovery at a
time. Therefore, the single-GPU lane keeps one receiver for all cells.

\noindent\textbf{Transport and Control.}
CUDA IPC rings connect processes on one GPU, and NVLink P2P rings connect GPUs. Each slot carries a
request version and a physical completion state. Copy completion does not imply receiver or recovery
completion; the owning worker publishes a separate completion event. The controller stores recovery
calendars, endpoint and transport credits, worker epochs, global AI ownership, and single-commit state.
Unix-domain RPC carries control messages, while CUDA-resident data stays on the IPC and P2P paths.

\noindent\textbf{Schedule Builder and Verifier.}
\sys separates schedule construction from admission authority. The candidate scheduler
applies deterministic list orders to radio jobs and visible AI candidates and emits a complete schedule,
an ownership delta, and a mode fingerprint; exact search serves only as a small-state oracle. The
independent verifier checks job identity, release, qualified service, expiry, lane capacity, overlap, AI busy intervals, credit uniqueness, and version, and the runtime cannot execute a candidate that the verifier
rejects. The executor performs the first non-preemptive action of an accepted schedule, observes its
terminal event, and replans; it never reorders physical work from an unverified host-side queue.

\noindent\textbf{Measurement and Mode Separation.}
Instrumentation separates logical decisions, host progress, GPU execution, and physical completion,
which prevents a host timestamp from being reported as a kernel boundary. The runtime records release,
admission, dispatch, worker observation, CUDA completion, commit return, request and payload identity,
ring version, worker epoch, and slot freeing time, and Nsight traces check GPU ordering for selected
modes. The qualified C158-C162 path uses pyAerial with persistent workers. A later native C++ effort
verifies input-layout and decoder parity only; it is not timing-qualified, and its measurements are never
mixed with the service bounds of the Python mode. Manifests preserve source hashes, protocols, seeds,
node exclusions, analyzer versions, failures, and result hashes, and every empirical block in this paper
maps to a machine-readable artifact and field set.

\subsection{Evaluation setup}
\label{sec:methodology}

\noindent\textbf{Hardware Specification.}
We evaluate \sys on two independent nodes, each with four NVIDIA A100-SXM4-40GB GPUs connected by
third-generation NVLink. The bursty-arrival, load, and single-GPU campaigns
(Sections~\ref{sec:eval-burst} and~\ref{sec:eval-single}) run on further nodes of the same system with four
A100-SXM4-80GB GPUs each; a single-GPU run uses one of the four GPUs. MIG is disabled and MPS is enabled
on every GPU. In the multi-GPU placement,
GPU0 and GPU1 run the two cuPHY instances at a 50\% MPS share, GPU3 runs the TensorRT \NRx worker at
80\%, and GPU2 runs the shared cuPHY recovery lane at 80\% and the Qwen worker at 20\% with low
priority. Facility identity is withheld for anonymous review.

\noindent\textbf{Evaluation Scope.}
The trace-driven evaluation (Sections~\ref{sec:eval-capacity} and~\ref{sec:eval-trace}) measures safe AI
capacity and timely AI value with an exact-checked capacity model and BurstGPT traces, without executing
kernels. The physical evaluation (Sections~\ref{sec:eval-online}-\ref{sec:eval-prediction}) runs the
complete scheduler on both nodes and measures online scheduling, AI service under bursty arrivals, the
single-GPU placement with early AI slots, radio
safety at the admission boundary, schedule enforcement under injected faults, prediction accuracy, and
decision cost. Section~\ref{sec:eval-sensitivity} varies the recovery bound,
the MPS configuration, and the worker lifecycle.

\noindent\textbf{Workloads and Contract.}
Radio execution uses NVIDIA Aerial cuPHY~\cite{aerial} and a TensorRT FP16 \NRx. The evaluated warm mode
uses a synthetic 180~ms release period and a 155~ms result expiry (\texttt{P180/D155}), with
finite-sample whole-path bounds $B_{\mathrm{NRx}}=45$~ms, $B_{\mathrm{conv}}=25$~ms, and
$B_{\mathrm{AI}}(\text{context64})=35$~ms and a 2~ms margin before the expiry. The period is not an NR slot, and the expiry
is not a production HARQ deadline. In every period, four admitted cells each send one single-UE, 273-PRB
uplink TB; the cuPHY instance on GPU0 serves three of them and the instance on GPU1 serves one.
Qwen2.5-1.5B prefill~\cite{qwen25} with 16-512-token prompts creates
external GPU contention, and BurstGPT~\cite{burstgpt} supplies arrivals and token values from four
non-overlapping public-trace windows and the prompts of the bursty physical campaign. Synthetic channels drive system qualification; a separate Sionna
CDL-D/E mode tests radio value (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\noindent\textbf{Baselines.}
Every baseline makes the same radio decisions and sees the same arrivals, bounds, and workers as \sys.
Two baselines follow the sharing approaches of Section~\ref{sec:related}. \emph{Static reservation}
holds the recovery of every admitted TB until expiry, as reservation-based sharing does, and
\emph{idle-time admission} admits AI into GPU time that is idle at the decision and ignores pending
recovery, as work-conserving sharing does. The \emph{recovery-first} policy is an ablation of \sys: it keeps pending-recovery
tracking, the all-fail admission check, and the single-commit rule
of \sys but runs every required recovery before external AI, which isolates the effect of AI-first
order. The \emph{offline oracle} sees the realized trace and bounds the attainable value. Idle-time admission and uncontrolled MPS
are diagnostic baselines; they expose broken assumptions and are not safe performance comparators.

\noindent\textbf{Experimental Discipline.}
Each authoritative campaign freezes its source hash, workload, bounds, seeds, node assignment,
exclusions, analyzer, and stop rule before opening the outcome. Development and holdout runs use
independent seeds and nodes, and failed, negative, and \UQ runs stay in the ledger. We report zero
observed events as finite-sample results rather than probability or worst-case execution time (WCET)
claims.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
The anonymous artifact package contains source, protocols, manifests, analyzers, exact checker,
independent verifier, result summaries, and figure builders. Hardware campaigns require proprietary
Aerial/cuPHY and TensorRT components; model, checker, trace, and analysis paths do not. Node names and
absolute paths are removed from the review artifact.

\paragraph{Use of generative AI.}
Generative-AI tools assisted with language editing, organization, bibliography normalization, and
consistency-checking scripts. The authors designed the system and model, fixed protocols, executed
experiments, inspected failures, and verified every claim against hashed artifacts. Generative AI
produced no experimental measurement or ground-truth label.

points, with a 10.64\% median positive gain. Because this oracle excludes movement cost and physical
qualification, it is a design target, and we make no executed optimizer claim. Selecting the frozen
Sionna strata to minimize pending recoveries under zero or 0.02 absolute radio-value loss removes no
pending recovery.

% provenance: C159_BASELINE
\noindent\textbf{Original Physical Mode.}
The advantage depends on the headroom that the workload leaves. The calibration-only exact screen of the
original physical mode gives static reservation, recovery-first, \sys, and the oracle the same radio
decisions, arrivals, bounds, and one-Qwen-per-period capacity. After correcting a preserved
absolute-deadline interpretation defect without excluding a request or opening the holdout, exact
matching assigns 856 timely requests and 349,387 tokens to recovery-first, \sys, and the oracle, and
static reservation exposes no qualified same-period Qwen slot. Charging every required recovery its full
bound leaves recovery-first at 345,405 tokens, only 1.14\% below \sys and below the prespecified 5\%
gate. Thus, the original mode contains no ordering headroom, and recovery-first
remains the strong safe baseline in that mode.

\subsection{Online scheduling on physical GPUs}
\label{sec:eval-online}

% provenance: Q2_WARM_PATH
\noindent\textbf{Two-Node Campaign.}
The frozen \texttt{P180/D155} campaign runs 600 release periods on each of two nodes and offers one Qwen
request of 16-512 tokens in every period. It executes 4,800 TensorRT \NRx requests, 1,312 shared-cuPHY
recoveries, 1,077 Qwen requests, and 4,800 single radio commits without a violation of any deadline, bound, credit,

Maximum \NRx, recovery, and radio-commit paths are 18.123, 13.465, and 118.022~ms against contracts of

physical co-execution rather than service bounds.

\subsection{AI service under bursty arrivals}
\label{sec:eval-burst}

% provenance: C176_BURST
\noindent\textbf{Bursty Workload.}
AI requests arrive in bursts, and during a burst the scheduler decides how much AI each recovery window
can absorb. Therefore, we replay one sequence of 329 BurstGPT prompts on the physical multi-GPU path at 3
requests/s for 108~s. The prompts are drawn from all BurstGPT requests and rounded up to the six prompt
lengths, and 47.4\% of them round to 512 tokens. Six arrival patterns share this sequence and differ only
in arrival times: evenly spaced arrivals, the per-second arrivals of a BurstGPT segment, and Gamma
arrivals with an inter-arrival CV of 1 (Poisson), 2, 4, and 8. Every request has a 200~ms SLO for its first
token, and up to four AI slots share one period in FIFO order. All policies of a pattern observe the same
\NRx outcome sequence, which makes each comparison paired.

\noindent\textbf{AI Service.}
Figure~\ref{fig:eval-burst}a shows the AI tokens served within their SLO. \sys serves 6.2-18.5\% more on-time
tokens than recovery-first under every pattern: 18.5\% for BurstGPT arrivals, 10.6\% for Poisson arrivals,
and 6.2\% for CV-8 bursts. Static reservation holds all four recoveries and serves no AI request in this

recoveries no longer fit before the recovery deadline. As the arrivals become burstier, every policy serves fewer
tokens because a burst exceeds the window of one period and its requests expire.

\noindent\textbf{Radio Safety under Bursts.}
Figure~\ref{fig:eval-burst}b shows the price of that extra AI. Idle-time admission misses the recovery deadline at the

after the recovery deadline in 2.7-9.7\% of periods, and 128 radio results miss the expiry. \sys and recovery-first
never miss the recovery deadline, and no radio result misses the expiry in any of their 18 runs. Under the same
padded execution, \sys serves 10.6-18.5\% more on-time tokens than recovery-first.

\noindent\textbf{Throughput under Load.}
Figure~\ref{fig:eval-load} raises the offered AI load with Poisson arrivals. With a 200~ms SLO, \sys serves
10.6-21.2\% more on-time AI tokens than recovery-first from 1.92 to 4.95 requests/s, and the two nearly tie
at 0.98 requests/s. Static reservation serves no AI request at any load, and idle-time admission serves
the most but breaks the recovery deadline at the declared bounds in 19-88 of 600 periods. With a
1,000~ms SLO (Figure~\ref{fig:eval-load}b), \sys and recovery-first reach the same throughput at every
load because both fit the same AI work and required recoveries into each window. Thus, tracking pending
recovery sets how much AI one window can hold, and AI-first order sets how much of it meets a tight
deadline.

\noindent\textbf{Where the Gain Comes From.}
Figure~\ref{fig:eval-burst}c varies the AI SLO in a replay with the measured service distributions of the
two-node campaign, averaged over five seeds. The gain of \sys over recovery-first shrinks as the SLO
loosens: 34.3\% on average at 150~ms, 14.2\% at 200~ms, 5.3\% at 300~ms, and 2.7\% at 500~ms. The measured
gains at 200~ms, 6.2-18.5\%, lie close to the replay range of 8.8-21.4\%. \sys achieves this gain because it
places a verified AI slot ahead of the required recoveries, while recovery-first must charge every required
recovery its full bound before an AI slot can meet its SLO. Thus, the ordering matters for interactive
AI SLOs and fades for loose ones, consistent with the tie of the original mode in
Section~\ref{sec:eval-trace}.

\noindent\textbf{Memory on One GPU.}
Memory limits the single-GPU placement before compute does. One cuPHY receiver of our harness holds

\noindent\textbf{Same Decisions on One GPU.}
Figure~\ref{fig:eval-single}a pairs the 30 natural runs on one GPU with their multi-GPU runs. The
on-time AI tokens match exactly in 27 pairs and differ by at most 3.6\% in the other three. Under
the bursty patterns, \sys serves 6.2-18.5\% more on-time tokens than recovery-first on both

1.7-10.0\% of periods on both. The placements agree because \sys separates the GPU in time: \NRx
runs before the \NRx deadline, and AI slots and recoveries run after it. Over the paired natural
runs, the p99 of every service path moves by at most 1.6~ms; for example, \NRx results arrive
within 6.7~ms against 7.0~ms.

\noindent\textbf{Tails on One GPU.}
One GPU leaves the medians and p99 unchanged but lengthens the tails
(Figure~\ref{fig:eval-single}b). Two of the 5,437 single-GPU prefills exceed their class bound: a
256-token request takes 77.6~ms against 65~ms, and a 512-token request takes 107.6~ms against 75~ms.
In the second case, the verifier finds at slot release that the remaining recovery no longer fits
before the recovery deadline, and the run stops. In addition, 5 of the 42 single-GPU runs lose a
\NRx outcome at the first cutoff after startup. In two of them, the owners stall for up to 0.35~s,
the first period's recoveries miss the deadline, 8 radio results miss the expiry, and the run stops.
None of the 39 multi-GPU runs shows any of these events. Thus, the single-GPU placement
is a separate mode: it needs a warm-up before the first release and bounds measured on one GPU, and
we report its runs as development evidence.

\noindent\textbf{Early AI Slots.}
On one GPU, an early AI slot shares the GPU with \NRx (Section~\ref{sec:design-recovery}). We pair \sys
with and without early slots under the same traces, on one node except for the CV-8 pair.
Figure~\ref{fig:eval-early}a shows that early slots serve 2.6-70.3\% more on-time AI tokens under the six
arrival patterns, and the gain grows with burstiness: a burst leaves short requests queued at the next
release, and an early slot serves them before the \NRx deadline. Under Poisson load, the gain is
5.4-14.5\% (Figure~\ref{fig:eval-early}c). Early slots do not delay \NRx: the p99 of the \NRx result

the GPU for its full bound (Figure~\ref{fig:eval-early}b), and no run loses a timely \NRx success. No
early-slot run misses the recovery deadline or the expiry. \sys achieves this gain because the all-fail
schedule already holds the recovery of every admitted TB at release. The planner skips slots that
cannot end in time before it calls the verifier, which keeps planning within the 5~ms launch budget when
a burst fills the queue.

\subsection{Schedule enforcement and fault containment}
\label{sec:eval-refinement}

\noindent\textbf{Latest-Start Enforcement.}
A committed schedule remains valid only if the GPU cannot start a slot later than the schedule assumes.
Figure~\ref{fig:eval-refinement}a shows the arrival of each slot at the Qwen worker relative to its
absolute latest-start. All 1,077 slots of the two-node campaign arrive 2.9-3.8~ms before the
latest-start and launch. The held-slot fault keeps 52 slots until 6.4-7.2~ms after the latest-start, and
the worker refuses all 52 before GPU launch with a non-launch marker. A launch that ignored the
latest-start would run these requests up to 7.2~ms later than their schedules allow and push the required
recoveries toward the recovery deadline. \sys achieves this by binding the absolute latest-start to the slot at
commit and by checking it in the worker immediately before kernel submission.

\noindent\textbf{Radio Commit Time.}
AI-first placement delays the required recoveries, and Figure~\ref{fig:eval-refinement}b measures this cost
on every TB of the two-node campaign. The 3,488 TBs with a timely \NRx success commit in the outcome
batch at a median of 46.1~ms after release, independent of the AI slot. Recovered TBs commit at a
median of 62.1~ms when no AI slot precedes the recovery, 72.7-73.7~ms after a 16-128-token slot,
86.0~ms after a 256-token slot, and 116.4~ms after a 512-token slot. The latest commit, 118.022~ms,
precedes the 155~ms expiry by 37.0~ms. Thus, AI-first placement moves a recovery commit later by at most
the scheduled slot length and never past the expiry, because the schedule charges each slot before
the recoveries that follow it.

% provenance: C160_FAULT
\noindent\textbf{Fault Containment.}
A stale message or a lost reply can free the wrong physical credit even when the schedule is
feasible. Therefore, we combine finite-state checking with physical fault injection. A finite-state
audit checks 51 combinations of worker epoch, version, completion, and marker state without an invariant
violation. The physical campaign injects seven fault classes on the two qualified nodes.
Figure~\ref{fig:eval-refinement}c shows that every TB in every class commits exactly once before
expiry. The largest commit time is 125.2~ms when every TB of a period fails and all four recoveries run,
and it stays 29.8~ms before the 155~ms expiry. The campaign executes 700
release periods, 2,800 \NRx requests, 942 recoveries, and 2,800 commits without an observed deadline
miss, including 40 replay pairs, four terminal faults, and 260 continuation periods; 0/2,800 misses
gives a 95\% rule-of-three upper bound of 0.107\%. One additional node violates the \NRx whole-path
bound on its first round, and we retain that run as \UQ without pooling it. Thus, fault containment
applies to the two qualified nodes and the declared fault and lifecycle classes.

\noindent\textbf{MPS Configuration.}
MPS controls concurrency but does not bound time. A separate-client overrun produces 2/1,500 deadline
misses at a 100\% cap and 1/1,500 at a 20\% cap, and a matched lifecycle comparison observes 0/10,000
misses for a CPU socket sham and 12/10,000 for the GPU/MPS-client arm. The two campaigns are reported
separately and do not imply a monotonic cap effect. Thus, neither the cap selection nor the current
queue state authorizes admission.

% provenance: C164_LIFECYCLE
\noindent\textbf{Worker Lifecycle.}
Worker lifecycle changes the service distribution on which a schedule relies. The lifecycle matrix
contains five qualified or partial subsets and five \UQ modes. Qualified evidence covers warm persistent
execution, the first request after 30~s idle, MPS restart followed by requalification, radio continuity
during Qwen reload, and same-worker reconnect. Optional-service availability during restart or reload
is outside the claim. Process and model cold starts, 5~min and 30~min idle, GC-on, and worker process
replacement remain \UQ. A single-node replacement result is ``Development canary only'' because it lacks
an independent holdout. Durable journal recovery would be a new mechanism and remains outside this
paper.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\noindent\textbf{GPU Sharing in AI-RAN and vRAN.}
Previous studies on AI-RAN and virtualized RAN (vRAN) platforms focus on sharing compute between
deadline-critical RAN functions and other workloads. YinYangRAN multiplexes 5G PHY and ML workloads on
GPU accelerators~\cite{yinyangran}, CloudRIC shares pools of heterogeneous accelerators among distributed
units~\cite{cloudric}, and Concordia reclaims vRAN CPU capacity while meeting processing
deadlines~\cite{concordia}. Other studies orchestrate AI and RAN workloads through MIG partitions,
heterogeneous placement, and worker location~\cite{interplay,caora,haf,distributedairan}, while ARCHES
switches PHY models, OCUDU exposes in-DU timing classes, and SMEC coordinates RAN and edge
SLOs~\cite{arches,ocudu,smec}.

Our study aligns with these efforts to reclaim RAN compute for other workloads. However, their scheduling
units are resources or queued jobs, and their public designs do not represent the recovery that a
speculative neural receiver may create. \sys bridges this gap by tracking each pending recovery and by
joining one all-fail recovery calendar across cuPHY instances to an external-AI slot that holds through
physical completion.

\noindent\textbf{Real-Time GPU Scheduling and Fault-Tolerant Reservation.}
Previous studies on GPU scheduling focus on predictable latency for concurrent GPU work through
microsecond-scale preemption~\cite{reef}, preemptive scheduling across accelerators~\cite{xsched},
spatio-temporal scheduling of real-time DNN inference~\cite{daris}, and hardware compute
partitioning~\cite{nvtaskset}. Fault-tolerant real-time scheduling reserves a backup copy for each primary
task, deallocates the backup when the primary succeeds, and overloads backups whose primaries cannot fail
together~\cite{ghosh1997primarybackup}. Imprecise computation splits a task into mandatory and optional
parts and schedules the optional part only when time remains~\cite{liu1991imprecise}.

While our study aligns with these reservation and preemption techniques, preemption and partitioning
control kernels that are already queued, and backup overloading assumes that primaries do not fail
together. \sys bridges this gap by verifying one all-fail schedule for the recovery of every pending receiver,
releasing a recovery on a timely success, and enforcing that schedule through GPU launch, transport
ownership, and physical completion.

\noindent\textbf{Scoped Difference.}
Across public designs reviewed through September 2026, we found no system that combines an executable
all-fail calendar for unresolved same-TB recovery, external-AI slots held until physical completion, and a
predictive admission limit qualified by measurement conditions. This is a scoped literature finding, not a claim about
unpublished work or other terminology.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\noindent\textbf{Claim Boundary.}
The guarantee is conditional on the qualified bounds and measurement conditions of the evaluated mode. \sys does
not claim that MPS provides hard isolation, that finite-sample maxima are WCETs, or that the synthetic
timing contract is a production HARQ deadline. Five lifecycle subsets are qualified or partially
qualified and five remain \UQ; the claim does not require or imply a 10/10 lifecycle matrix.

\noindent\textbf{Timing Contract.}
\texttt{P180/D155} is a synthetic harness contract: $P$ is the inter-release period, not an NR slot
duration, and \texttt{D155} is not a production HARQ deadline. A production \texttt{d\_MAC} trace
builder and validator pass 24 unit tests, but a target DU/FAPI trace is unavailable. A new vector
requires physical requalification; we make no production HARQ guarantee.

% provenance: P2_FAST_PATH_AND_STAGE
\noindent\textbf{Production Timing.}
Aerial testMAC expects a CRC-bearing UL indication by $T0+4.5$~ms. Under clean, warm, no-AI conditions,
local wait-then-recover, same-GPU speculative, and precomputed cross-GPU paths complete 0/1,000 within
4.5~ms. Raw-IQ P2P moves channel estimation through CRC to GPU1 and reaches 852/1,000, and a shared
cuPHY/TensorRT stream reaches 885/1,000. The frozen persistent, stream-ordered fast path reaches
995/1,000 with a 3.813~ms median and a 3.995~ms p99, but five late completions reject the gate, and the
holdout stays closed. A 300-request profile attributes the remaining tail to remote channel estimation:
GPU p50/p99/max are 0.866/4.873/9.592~ms, and its correlation with pair wall time is 0.9603. P2P copies
average 36.261/17.150~$\mu$s, derate matching exposes a 1,060.105~$\mu$s host call despite a 0.168~ms
GPU p99, TensorRT enqueue averages 7.443~$\mu$s, and the TensorRT graph has a 0.886~ms GPU p99. These
profiles are mechanism evidence rather than qualification or WCET. TestMAC is a controlled developer L2
rather than a field-DU $d_{\mathrm{MAC}}$ source.

% provenance: P3_CHANNEL_HOLDOUT
\noindent\textbf{Channel Scope.}
External Aerial TDL-A remains unsupported. Across raw and normalized inputs, public-reference radio
settings, one-antenna frequency/time channel execution, and a public-notebook-style FP32 engine,
conventional decoding succeeds in every ten-TB arm and \NRx succeeds in none. A fixed development matrix
selects Sionna CDL-D/E at 100~ns and retains the A/B/C failures, and a disjoint-seed holdout uses five
Es/No strata and 50 blocks per stratum per model. Both pipelines pass 50/50 at 10~dB. CDL-D yields
153/250 conventional and 164/250 neural successes, with 16 neural-only and five conventional-only
outcomes, and CDL-E yields 155/250 and 163/250, with 15 neural-only and seven conventional-only
outcomes. The aggregate 31 neural-only versus 12 conventional-only outcomes give a two-sided exact
$p=0.00540$: optional \NRx has conditional radio value in the frozen Sionna CDL-D/E mode. That channel
has not passed production timing or integrated service-vector qualification.

\noindent\textbf{Finite-Sample Qualification.}
Zero observed violations establish neither a WCET nor a zero failure probability. Bounds belong to one
hardware, software, placement, workload, and lifecycle fingerprint. All authoritative hardware results
use A100s, and NVLink P2P does not establish cross-family or inter-node data-plane validity.

% provenance: C158_LONG_TAIL
\noindent\textbf{Unresolved Long Tail.}
In C158 attempt 4, one \NRx completion reached 350.948\,ms while the 400-request run's p99 was
8.111\,ms; the run had 4/400 deadline misses. Targeted idle, MPS restart and requalification, Qwen
reload, and reconnect campaigns did not reproduce it. Its cause remains unresolved, and GPU and driver
hangs remain outside the fault model.

\noindent\textbf{Workload Mapping.}
BurstGPT supplies real arrivals and token lengths, and Qwen2.5-1.5B prefill is a representative external
workload. Token buckets and AI deadlines are experimental mappings; other workloads need new bounds and
replay qualification.

\section{Conclusion}

In this paper, we present \sys, a recovery-safe multi-GPU scheduler that shares AI-RAN GPUs
between neural receivers and AI inference. By
tracking each pending recovery and admitting AI only with an executable all-fail schedule, \sys keeps
radio recovery within its deadline while it reclaims the GPU capacity that neural-receiver success
releases. Our evaluation shows that \sys reaches the oracle AI capacity across 13,716 feasible model
points and delivers a median of 1.44$\times$ the timely AI value of recovery-first
at the trace headroom points. On two four-GPU nodes, \sys records 0/120 safety violations in boundary cases
where idle-time admission violates 40/40, serves 6.2-18.5\% more on-time AI tokens than recovery-first
under bursty AI arrivals, and commits every TB exactly once under seven injected fault classes. On one
GPU, early AI slots add 2.6-70.3\% more on-time AI tokens without losing a timely \NRx result. These results qualify a synthetic timing mode, not
production HARQ timing.
